Overall Call

Sideways

Monday sets up as a continuation of the range, because the two forces holding this market are pulling almost exactly against each other. On one side, domestic institutions keep absorbing whatever foreign investors sell — Friday's ₹2,076 crore of DII buying comfortably covered ₹667 crore of FII selling, and that pattern has been the floor under the Nifty for weeks. On the other, Brent at roughly $88 with the Strait of Hormuz still closed is a direct tax on an economy that imports most of its crude, and it caps any attempt to break higher.

The macro backdrop reinforces the stalemate rather than resolving it. Wall Street took a record high on Wednesday and then gave a little back on Friday, closing the third straight positive week — constructive, but visibly tired. And the Fed debate has inverted: after this week's in-line inflation prints, roughly 69% of the CME FedWatch probability sits on a September hold with the remaining 31% on a hike. A cut is not in the conversation. That removes the liquidity tailwind emerging markets normally lean on, without yet delivering the shock that would force a repricing.

Nifty closed Friday at 24,366. The working range is 24,200–24,250 on support and 24,500–24,550 on resistance, and nothing in the weekend calendar obviously breaks it. Expect a quiet, stock-specific Monday with the real event risk arriving midweek.

What flips the call. To bullish: a credible Iran–Oman breakthrough on reopening Hormuz that pushes Brent back toward the low $80s or below — that single move fixes India's import bill, the rupee and the inflation path at once; failing that, a decisive Nifty close above 24,550 on strong volume with FIIs turning net buyers. To bearish: fresh attacks on shipping or a hard breakdown in the talks taking Brent through $95, which would revive the September hike bet and hit Indian equities twice over; or an FOMC minutes release on Wednesday that reads more hawkish than the market's 31% hike probability implies. A sustained break of 24,200 with FII selling accelerating would confirm it.

Global Markets

Wall Street ended the week with a small pullback from a record. The S&P 500 slipped about 0.2% on Friday after setting an all-time high on Wednesday, but still logged a third consecutive weekly gain. The Dow fell roughly 108 points, or 0.2%, to about 53,732, and the Nasdaq Composite eased around 0.3% while eking out a marginal weekly rise. The proximate cause was the University of Michigan's preliminary August consumer sentiment reading, which showed households turning more downbeat with inflation still front of mind.

Asia was split rather than directional. Europe closed mildly lower, with the FTSE the weakest of the majors — energy-heavy indices did not get the lift from firm crude that one might expect, which tells you the oil bid is being read as a risk premium rather than a demand signal.

Risk sentiment reads as cautiously constructive with a live geopolitical hedge underneath — equities near highs, but gold near highs too. Those two things are not usually true at the same time.

Friday was volatile but went nowhere, which has become the house style. Selling clustered in auto, energy, FMCG, metals, pharma and realty; consumer durables and media held up. The index gave back a fraction of a percent and closed inside the same band it has occupied for the better part of a fortnight.

The flow picture is the more useful signal. Domestic institutions outbought foreign selling by roughly three to one on Friday. As long as that holds, downside is cushioned — but it is a defensive floor, not an engine, and it does not by itself produce a breakout. Buyers have repeatedly defended the lower end of the range while failing to hold above the 24,650–24,750 zone, which is the honest description of a market waiting for a trigger it has not been given.

Levels for Monday: support 24,200–24,250, resistance 24,500–24,550. Note the absence of a GIFT Nifty read this morning — without it, the opening gap is genuinely unforecast, and any Sunday developments on Hormuz will matter more than usual to the first hour.

Both benchmarks rose on Friday, with Brent around $88 after touching close to $90 midweek. The driver is singular and unresolved: Iran and Oman have not reached agreement on reopening the Strait of Hormuz, and Tehran's foreign ministry has stated that the US naval blockade must be lifted before the conditions for a full reopening exist. US officials, meanwhile, say they are expanding their capacity to escort vessels through the strait — a workaround, not a settlement.

For context on how much of this price is conflict premium: Brent is up roughly 24% versus the level before the US–Israel campaign against Iran began in late February. The EIA does not expect Middle East production to return to near pre-conflict levels until early 2027 and projects Brent averaging about $87 across 2026. One analyst's framing captures the market's position well — hope for a deal has not been abandoned, but confidence is eroding.

Gold

Gold held above $4,400 an ounce into the weekend and is carrying a monthly gain of more than 10%. Reported Friday spot levels ranged from about $4,373 to $4,420 across providers through the session — treat $4,400 as the reference level rather than any single print.

The drivers are a softer dollar and this week's in-line US inflation readings, which pushed the market toward expecting the Fed to hold in September. Worth being precise about the direction of that repricing: the debate has been between a hold and a hike, not between a hold and a cut, and gold has rallied anyway. That is a safe-haven bid doing the work rather than a rate-differential one, and the obvious source is Hormuz. Central bank buying remains a structural support underneath.

The practical read for Monday is that gold and equities are both near highs, which means the market is long risk and long insurance simultaneously. If Hormuz resolves, expect gold to give back more than equities gain.

NSE Earnings Highlights

The Q1 FY27 season is effectively over. Friday 14 August was the SEBI filing deadline, so there is no meaningful earnings calendar for the coming week — one reason Monday is likely to be thin on stock-specific catalysts. This week's late filers included Hindustan Aeronautics, Ashok Leyland, JSW Cement and Affle 3i.

The aggregate picture from the season is worth carrying into next week, because it explains the index's reluctance to break higher. Revenue growth across the Nifty 50 has been healthy, pointing to resilient underlying demand. Operating profit growth has followed. But net profit growth has been notably softer, with rising costs compressing margins. Industrials, automobiles, energy and financial services were among the stronger sectors.

A market where the top line grows and the bottom line does not is a market that struggles to justify multiple expansion. With crude at $88 feeding straight into input costs, that margin squeeze is unlikely to reverse on its own in the current quarter.
