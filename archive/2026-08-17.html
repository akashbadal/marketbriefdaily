Overall Call

Sideways — downside tilt

The range holds, but India is now sitting at the wrong end of it, and today’s most useful signal is a divergence rather than a direction. Asia is broadly firm this morning — Hong Kong up over 1.5%, Korea up more than 2%, Shanghai higher — while the Nifty opened lower and stayed lower. When the region rallies and India does not, the explanation is usually local, and today it is the oil bill. Brent is pressing $90 with the Strait of Hormuz still closed, which is a direct import-cost tax that Hong Kong and Seoul simply do not pay in the same way.

GIFT Nifty had flagged this: futures were down about 50 points to 24,399 ahead of the open, and the cash market delivered. By mid-morning the Nifty was near 24,294, down roughly 0.3%, with the Sensex off about 0.4% at 77,671 and briefly 400 points lower at the day’s trough. Breadth was no better — midcaps and smallcaps were both negative. JSW Steel, Dr Reddy’s and Jio Financial led the losers.

The rate backdrop offers no rescue either. After last week’s in-line US inflation prints the September debate has settled into hold-versus-hike, not hold-versus-cut. That removes the liquidity tailwind emerging markets usually lean on. Wednesday’s FOMC minutes are the week’s real event, and until they land there is little reason for anyone to take a large directional position.

What flips the call. To bullish: genuine movement on Hormuz — a lifting of the naval blockade or a credible reopening framework — that drops Brent back toward the low $80s; that single change repairs the import bill, the rupee and the inflation path together. Absent that, a reclaim of 24,500–24,550 on strong volume with sustained FII buying. To bearish: Brent through $95 on fresh shipping attacks, or a decisive break of 24,200 that turns the fortnight-long floor into resistance. FOMC minutes reading more hawkish than the market’s current hike odds would do the same job by Wednesday evening.

Global Markets

Wall Street is the settled part of the picture. Friday closed with a modest pullback from Wednesday’s record: the S&P 500 slipped about 0.2% to roughly 7,786, the Nasdaq Composite eased around 0.3% to about 26,729, and the Dow gave up roughly 0.2% to near 53,732. Weaker-than-expected retail sales and a soft University of Michigan sentiment print did the damage. Even so, all three logged a third consecutive weekly gain — constructive, but visibly tiring.

That is a risk-on tape — and it is happening despite oil near $90, which tells you the region is trading its own semiconductor and China-policy narrative rather than the Gulf. Risk sentiment reads as regionally split: constructive in North Asia, defensive in India, and hedged everywhere — gold near record territory while equities sit near highs remains the uncomfortable combination underneath all of it.

Brent has been holding around $90 with the market reporting little progress toward ending the Iran conflict. The blockage is specific and unresolved: the memorandum of understanding signed on 17 June to open Hormuz to commercial traffic collapsed within weeks over which routes vessels could use, and Iran’s foreign ministry has since stated that as long as the US naval blockade continues, the conditions for a full reopening do not exist. Washington’s answer has been to expand naval escorts — a workaround, not a settlement.

To size the risk premium: Brent is up roughly 24% against pre-conflict levels from late February. The EIA does not expect Middle East production to recover to near pre-conflict levels before early 2027 and projects Brent averaging around $87 across 2026 — meaning current prices sit above the agency’s own full-year path.

Levels here are approximate and deliberately quoted as a range. Providers disagreed by more than a dollar depending on contract month and timestamp; treat “around $90” as the reference rather than any single print.

Gold

Gold is trading near $4,420 an ounce, with providers clustering roughly between $4,390 and $4,420 — approximate, as always with spot. That extends a monthly gain of more than 10% and keeps bullion in the upper reaches of its range.

Two drivers are doing the work. Softer US data has lowered September Fed hike bets, easing Treasury yields and weakening the dollar, which mechanically supports a non-yielding asset. Underneath that sits a persistent safe-haven bid tied to the Gulf, plus the structural central-bank accumulation that has underpinned the metal for two years.

The read-across for Indian equities is the one worth holding onto: gold near highs and global equities near highs at the same time means the market is long risk and long insurance simultaneously. That is not a stable configuration. If Hormuz resolves, gold likely gives back more than equities gain; if it deteriorates, the reverse trade is already crowded.

NSE Earnings Highlights

The Q1 FY27 season is effectively closed. Friday 14 August was the SEBI filing deadline, so there is no meaningful large-cap earnings calendar this week. That is part of why today is trading on macro rather than stock-specific news — there is very little company-level flow left to react to.

The aggregate season read is the thing to carry forward, because it explains the index’s reluctance to break higher. Nifty 50 revenue growth was healthy, pointing to resilient underlying demand, and operating profit followed. But net profit growth was notably softer, with rising costs compressing margins. Industrials, automobiles, energy and financial services were among the stronger sectors.

A market where the top line grows and the bottom line does not is a market that struggles to justify multiple expansion. With Brent near $90 feeding directly into input costs, that margin squeeze has no obvious self-correction in the current quarter.
