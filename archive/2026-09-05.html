Overall Call

Constructive open, sideways after

Monday should open higher, and the reason is mechanical rather than a change of view. The Nifty closed Friday at 23,897.70, up a token 24 points, snapping a four-session losing streak. Ninety minutes after that bell, US August payrolls came in at +162,000 against a consensus near 53,000 — a three-fold beat — with unemployment steady at 4.1%. Wall Street took it as growth confirmation and rallied: S&P +1.1%, Dow +1.2%, Nasdaq +1.4%. India has that gain still to absorb.

But the same print revived the hike trade, and that is the catch. A labour market adding 162,000 jobs when the twelve-month average is nearer 31,000 hands the hawks their argument three weeks before the 16 September FOMC. Short-term futures moved to price a September move at better than 60%. US equities can rally on strong growth and still leave an emerging market carrying a heavier dollar and a firmer rate path. India imports that second effect without the first.

Flows say domestic money is doing the work. On Friday FIIs sold ₹3,112 crore in the cash segment while DIIs bought ₹8,930 crore — near three rupees of domestic buying for every rupee of foreign selling. That is what has been holding the index up through a four-day slide, and it is a real support. It is also a reminder that the marginal foreign buyer is still absent while crude sits in the mid-$90s and the rupee sits near record lows.

The quiet complication: US markets are closed Monday for Labor Day. No Wall Street cue arrives during or after Monday’s Indian session, and global liquidity will be thin. That argues for a positive open on Friday’s carry-over, then a drift — there is no fresh external input to sustain a trend, and nobody builds size into a holiday tape. India VIX at roughly 10.7, down almost 6% on Friday, is consistent with a market that expects a range rather than a move.

What flips the call. To bullish: the Nifty taking and holding 24,000–24,100 with breadth behind it, Bank Nifty accepting above 57,600, FIIs turning net buyers on the catch-up, Brent slipping under $88 on any Hormuz de-escalation, or the rupee recovering off its lows. To bearish: a sustained break of 23,800, Brent decisively through $100, a fresh Gulf escalation headline during Indian hours, the rupee sliding past 96, or hike odds for 16 September firming toward 80% on hawkish Fed commentary. Confirming sideways: a gap up of 80–150 points that fails to extend, crude holding $93–96 without new headlines, and volume fading through the afternoon into a shut US market — the base case.

Friday snapped the four-day losing streak, but only just. The Sensex added 0.48% while the Nifty managed 0.10% — a heavyweight-led move rather than a broad one. Nifty Metal was the top gainer; Realty the top laggard. Bank Nifty was essentially flat, down 0.02%, which is the detail that keeps this from being a clean reversal. Smallcaps, however, hit a fresh high — the same rotation into breadth that has run all week.

The flow picture is the most informative number in this briefing. FIIs sold ₹3,112 crore on Friday (gross purchases ₹13,858 crore against sales ₹16,970 crore); DIIs bought ₹8,930 crore (₹19,254 crore bought against ₹10,324 crore sold). Domestic institutions are absorbing foreign selling comfortably. That is why the index has held 23,800 through a losing week — and it is also why a genuine breakout probably needs FIIs to stop selling, not just DIIs to keep buying.

Levels for Monday. Immediate support at 23,800; the first meaningful recovery and confirmation zone is 24,000–24,100. On the banks, Bank Nifty support at 57,300, with 57,600–57,800 the zone needing sustained acceptance. Analyst framing into the week is cautious: aggressive long positions carry above-average risk until the index proves itself above 24,000.

The rupee remains the external constraint. It has been trading near record lows against the dollar through the oil shock, and Friday’s strong US payrolls print is dollar-supportive at the margin. A specific Friday close was not verified for this edition — treat the currency as a risk factor to check at Monday’s open rather than a number quoted here.

Crude Oil

Brent around $95, WTI around $91 — Brent slipped 0.31% Friday to roughly $95.23, WTI 0.11% to about $91.20. Feeds differ by a dollar or more on the same timestamp; “Brent in the mid-$90s” is the statement that survives cross-checking.

The driver is still military rather than economic. US strikes against Iran resumed this week after roughly a month of relative calm, with Tehran retaliating against American bases in the region and vessels transiting Hormuz. Prices have consolidated at an elevated level rather than faded, which is what distinguishes a supply-channel event from a headline spike.

For India this is the central macro problem. An economy importing the large majority of its crude at mid-$90s Brent faces a widening current-account deficit, currency pressure, and margin compression in oil-sensitive sectors — simultaneously, and regardless of what the Fed decides on 16 September. Any Monday move in the Nifty that ignores crude should be treated with suspicion.

Gold

Spot gold roughly $4,400–4,470. Three reputable feeds put Friday between $4,402.89 (morning ET), $4,443.53 (evening ET) and $4,470.66. Some of that is intraday movement and some is genuine provider disagreement; no single figure here should be read as precise. Directionally, gold firmed over the week from roughly $4,374 mid-week.

Gold is caught between its two drivers, pulling opposite ways. Safe-haven demand from the Gulf escalation argues higher; a Fed that might hike — raising the opportunity cost of a non-yielding asset — argues lower. Friday’s payrolls beat strengthened the second force, which is why gold did not extend meaningfully despite an equity rally and live geopolitical risk.

The swing factor is next week’s US inflation data, not the jobs number. If August CPI comes in cool, the Fed can discount the hot labour print, hike odds fall and gold gets its cleanest run in weeks. If CPI is warm alongside +162,000 payrolls, a September hike becomes close to consensus and gold has a problem.

Major Events to Watch (India & USA)

  • US markets closed Monday 7 September — Labor Day. Equities and bonds shut, reopening Tuesday 8 September. No overnight US cue for Tuesday’s Indian open, and thin global liquidity through Monday.
  • US August CPI — next week, ahead of the FOMC. Now the single most important item on the calendar. Morgan Stanley’s Ellen Zentner framed it directly: a hot payrolls print ramps up hike concerns, but the outcome sits with the inflation numbers — a cool CPI lets the Fed discount the labour-market signal.
  • FOMC, 15–16 September. Rates at 3.50–3.75%. The live question remains a hike, not a cut. Probabilities have swung repeatedly — recent reads range from roughly 40% to above 60% for a September move depending on source and date. Treat any single number as stale.
  • US–Iran / Strait of Hormuz. The dominant two-way tail risk. A new strike or a credible de-escalation moves crude, the rupee and Indian sentiment inside a single session.
  • Political noise around the Fed. President Trump publicly called the August report a “great jobs number” while pressing the Fed to cut rather than hike. Rhetoric of this kind has not moved policy, but it does move intraday rate expectations.
  • RBI. Policy rate held at 5.25% for a fifth consecutive meeting. No scheduled decision Monday; watch for intervention if the rupee sets fresh lows on dollar strength.
