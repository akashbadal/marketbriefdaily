Overall Call

Bearish — gap-down, respect the CPI overnight

The change from yesterday is the magnitude of the Asian move, and it is a big change. For most of this week Asia was drifting down half a percent to a percent and India was outperforming on domestic flows. This morning Japan’s Nikkei 225 is down about 3% and Korea’s Kospi about 2.6%. Those are not drift numbers. That is a regional de-risking session, and it removes the cushion that let the Nifty close green on Thursday. GIFT Nifty at 23,348, down 137 points, points to an open in the 23,300–23,350 band — straight into the target zone yesterday’s page named.

Crude remains the engine and it has not stopped. WTI is through $102 and Brent is above $101, with one feed as high as $106.74 (see the caveat above). The driver is unchanged and escalating: US–Iran fighting around the Strait of Hormuz and Houthi strikes on Saudi energy assets and shipping. For India this is the worst possible single variable — an importer of the overwhelming majority of its crude faces an inflation problem, a current-account problem and a margin problem simultaneously. A fourth consecutive Wall Street down day was explicitly attributed to oil topping $100 and fears of inflation from a prolonged Middle East war.

Wall Street gave no cover, and bonds are the aggravating factor. The S&P 500 fell 0.58% to 7,591.70, the Nasdaq 0.65% to 26,081.72 and the Dow 0.6% (316.56 points) to 52,064.10 — a fourth straight decline, with bond yields jumping. High energy prices plus rising yields is the specific combination that drains emerging-market risk appetite. Note one small counterweight: US futures are marginally positive (Dow +0.19%, S&P +0.11%), which argues the American market is not yet in panic even as Asia is.

Domestic flows are still the floor, and they are still holding — just barely more thinly. FIIs sold a modest ₹438.24 crore in cash on Thursday against DII buying of ₹1,025.85 crore. Domestic money continues to absorb foreign selling comfortably; this is a macro grind, not distribution. But the derivatives book is more negative than the cash book: FII index futures −₹857 crore and stock futures −₹1,629 crore, against index options +₹4,132 crore. Foreign desks are hedging and shorting the index while the cash tape looks calm. That divergence is the tell.

The one thing that makes today different from any other day this week: US August CPI prints at 18:00 IST — 8:30 ET, after the Indian close. Nobody with a choice will carry large risk into a Friday close ahead of the most-watched inflation number in the world, in a week when oil went through $100. Expect thin volumes, a reluctance to add longs into the afternoon, and the real reaction on Monday rather than today.

Net: bias down, conviction moderate — deliberately not high, because Thursday’s green close proved DII absorption is stronger than the tape suggests, and because the most important number of the week lands after Indian hours. 23,400 is now resistance rather than support; the zone to watch below is 23,200–23,300, and beneath that 23,000 becomes the round-number magnet. A gap-down that fills and reclaims 23,400 would be a genuine sign of resilience.

What flips the call. To bullish: Brent back below $95 on any credible Hormuz de-escalation or safe-passage deal; the Nifty filling the gap and closing back above 23,450–23,500; or Asia recovering intraday from its 3% lows, which would recast this as a one-off flush. To more decisively bearish: a sustained break of 23,200, Brent confirming near $106 or pushing to $110 on an actual supply interruption rather than a risk premium, or the Nikkei closing at its lows and dragging the whole region into a second session of the same. Confirming the base case: a gap-down that neither fills nor extends, crude holding $101–103, and a listless afternoon as desks refuse to position before the CPI print. The honest asymmetry today is that the market’s real answer comes at 18:00 IST, not at 15:30.

Global Markets

The US session was an oil session for the fourth day in a row. Wall Street fell across the board on Thursday as US crude topped $100 a barrel and bond yields jumped, with the declines explicitly tied to inflation fears from a prolonged Middle East war. The spread of the losses is worth noting: at −0.58% / −0.65% / −0.6%, the three indices moved almost identically. The market has stopped discriminating between cyclicals and tech and is simply reducing gross exposure. Four straight down days makes this a trend rather than a wobble.

Asia is where today’s story actually is. Asia-Pacific markets are trading sharply lower, with the Nikkei 225 down roughly 3% and the Kospi down about 2.6% as traders weigh the inflation outlook against high oil and advancing bond yields. This is a step-change in magnitude from the 1–1.5% declines earlier in the week. Hong Kong and mainland China could not be confirmed at the time of writing and are a gap in this edition.

US futures are the one dissenting voice. Dow futures +0.19% and S&P futures +0.11% are mildly positive despite the weak overnight cash session. That is not a bullish signal, but it does suggest the American market views the Asian move as a regional inflation-and-yields reaction rather than a global shock. If Asia is right and futures are wrong, today gets worse; if futures are right, Asia stabilises into the European open.

Europe is a look-ahead. The FTSE 100 and DAX open after this briefing, inheriting a sharply falling Asian tape, crude above $100, rising yields and a US CPI print four hours after their open. On those inputs the bias is lower, with energy names the likely sole bright spot and the FTSE relatively better placed than the DAX given its energy weighting. Crude Oil

WTI is through $102 and Brent is above $101 — with an unresolved spread on Brent that matters. WTI is quoted around $102.59. Brent prints $101.25 on one feed for Thursday and $106.74 as a current level on another. A $5.50 disagreement on the day’s dominant variable is too wide to average away, so this page reports it as unresolved: Brent is above $101 and may be considerably higher. Verify on a live feed before trading off it.

The driver is escalation, not fundamentals. Both benchmarks are above $100 on US–Iran fighting and Iran-backed Houthi forces targeting Saudi assets and threatening shipping routes. This is a risk-premium move around the Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly a fifth of world seaborne petroleum trade passes. The distinction matters for positioning: a risk premium can unwind in a single headline, whereas an actual supply interruption would not. Nothing so far confirms a physical outage.

For India this is the transmission channel for everything else. Triple-digit crude hits the import bill, the rupee, inflation expectations and the margins of every downstream and transport-exposed business. Oil marketing companies, paints, tyres, aviation and logistics are the direct casualties; upstream producers are the hedge. Every other section of this briefing is, in some sense, a second-order effect of this one.

Gold

Gold is holding near record territory around $4,390 an ounce, doing exactly what it is supposed to do. Feeds print between $4,365 and $4,406; it traded above $4,390 mid-week. Call the level roughly $4,390, approximate. Technical commentary frames the near-term range as $4,255–$4,577, which is a wide band and a fair reflection of how unsettled the tape is.

Three supports, pulling in the same direction. A softer US dollar; safe-haven demand from escalating Middle East tensions; and renewed inflation risk from the oil move. There is a genuine cross-current here worth naming: the same inflation fear raising rate-hike expectations ahead of the Fed meeting is usually a headwind for gold, since higher real rates raise the cost of holding a non-yielding asset. So far the geopolitical bid is winning. Underneath the tactical picture, central bank buying continues as more institutions diversify reserves away from the dollar amid trade friction — a structural, price-insensitive source of demand.

For Indian investors the rupee-denominated move will exceed the dollar move if the currency softens on the oil bill, which compounds the gain locally. Gold holding up while equities gap down is confirmation the market is pricing genuine risk aversion, not a technical wobble.

Major Events to Watch (India & USA)

US August CPI — today, 8:30 ET / 18:00 IST. This is the event. The Bureau of Labor Statistics releases August CPI today, and the timing is awkward for Indian traders: it lands roughly two and a half hours after the NSE close. The read-through is that today’s Indian session is a positioning session, not a reaction session — the reaction is Monday’s. A hot print, with oil above $100 feeding through, would harden expectations that the Fed stays higher for longer and keep pressure on emerging-market equities. A soft print is the single most plausible source of a relief rally next week.

  • Middle East / Strait of Hormuz (ongoing, both sides). The live variable. US–Iran confrontation and Houthi strikes on Saudi energy infrastructure and shipping are what put crude through $100. Any ceasefire, safe-passage arrangement or de-escalation headline is the fastest route to an oil-led relief rally; any confirmed physical supply outage is the fastest route to $110.
  • Fed expectations (US). Rate-hike expectations are being discussed ahead of the next Fed meeting — a meaningful shift in the conversation, and one that explains the jump in yields better than growth data does. Watch the tone of Fed commentary after the CPI print.
  • Bond yields (US). Yields jumped on Thursday and are now an independent driver rather than a follower of oil. Sustained higher yields tighten conditions for Indian equities regardless of what crude does next.
  • Rupee and the import bill (India). Not a scheduled event, but with crude above $100 the currency becomes a daily watch item and a channel through which the oil move reaches domestic inflation and RBI thinking.
  • Weekend gap risk (India). Friday plus a major US data release after hours plus an active geopolitical conflict is a combination that argues for smaller overnight positions than usual into the close.

No India-specific scheduled macro release or policy decision was confirmed for today inside the research window. That is a gap rather than a confirmed absence.
