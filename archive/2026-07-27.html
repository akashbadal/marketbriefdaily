Strikes paused, crude cracks 5% — Nifty set for a relief open

Monday, 27 July 2026 · Prepared before the 09:15 IST open · US levels are Friday's close; Asian levels are live

Written at ~08:30 IST. Wall Street closed hours ago and those numbers are final. Asian markets are one to two hours into their session, so those moves will drift. Europe has not opened.

Overall Call

Cautiously bullish open · sideways beyond that

Expect a gap-up and then a fight at resistance. The single driver is geopolitical: the US paused strikes on Iran for a second straight day on Sunday and Tehran reciprocated, with Oman mediating on Strait of Hormuz transit rules. That knocked roughly 5% off crude in one move — Brent to about $91.70, WTI to about $84.40 — which is exactly the pressure valve Indian equities needed. GIFT Nifty is up 139 points at 23,945.50, pointing to a ~0.6% higher start.

Why not fully bullish. Three things cap it. The Nifty closed Friday at 23,767 after five straight losing sessions, still below the 50-DMA/100-DMA confluence around 23,850 — a gap-up straight into that band is a seller's zone until it is reclaimed on a closing basis. FIIs sold ₹3,893 cr on Friday and the US 10-year at ~4.63% keeps emerging-market flows tight. And the pause is a pause, not a treaty; the same headline that gave this rally can take it back.

Add to that the calendar: the Fed decides on Wednesday 29 July and it is monthly F&O expiry/rollover week, so intraday noise will be above normal regardless of direction.

What flips the call: turns properly bullish if Nifty closes above 23,850 and then clears 24,000–24,100, Brent holds below $90 on a formal Hormuz agreement, and the Fed reads dovish on Wednesday. Turns bearish if the gap-up is sold and Nifty loses 23,600 — that opens 23,550 and then 23,300 — or if strikes resume and Brent snaps back above $100, or if FII selling accelerates alongside a further rise in US yields.

This is the story of the day. The lull in fighting around the Strait of Hormuz sent Brent down about 5.2% to roughly $91.73 and US crude down about 5.4% to roughly $84.45. Feeds disagree on the exact print — one wire has WTI extending toward $83.80 and Brent down more than $10 from its recent high — so treat these as approximate levels in a fast-moving market rather than settled closes. Directionally there is no ambiguity: this is a sharp, one-sided unwind of the war premium built up over the past fortnight, when Brent briefly touched $100.

What actually changed. The US halted strikes on Iranian coastal targets for a second consecutive day and Iran stopped firing at vessels transiting Hormuz. Talks continue through Oman, and the compromise under discussion is Iran policing transit through the Strait itself with fewer restrictions on shipping. That is a plausible deal but not a signed one.

For India this matters more than any domestic datapoint. Every $10 off Brent eases the import bill, the current account deficit and the inflation path, and restores some of the RBI's room to cut. Oil marketing companies, paints, aviation, tyres and autos are the direct beneficiaries; upstream producers and oil & gas names give back last week's gains.

Gold rose about 1.4% to roughly $4,110 an ounce — which looks counterintuitive on a day when war risk receded, and is worth explaining. The driver was not safe-haven demand; it was rates and the dollar. Lower oil pushed the US 10-year down 4 basis points to 4.63% and softened the dollar index to 101.23, and non-yielding gold benefits directly from both. Quoted levels vary by provider between roughly $4,070 and $4,110 depending on timestamp, so the level is approximate.

The tension to watch this week: de-escalation removes the geopolitical bid under gold, but a dovish Fed on Wednesday would replace it with a rates bid. Those two forces pull in opposite directions, which argues for a choppy rather than a trending week in bullion.
