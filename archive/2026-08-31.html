Overall Call

Bearish tilt — gap-down open, then a range fight

Two things changed over the weekend and both point the same way. First, US forces struck two Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island on Sunday, hit as they prepared to fire sea mines into the Strait of Hormuz. It is the first US strike on Iran since late July, and Iran is reported to have responded against US positions in Jordan. Second, and less dramatically but more durably, the market is still digesting Fed Chair Kevin Warsh’s hawkish Jackson Hole keynote, which pushed September rate-hike odds above even money. Oil rising into a hawkish Fed is the least friendly combination Indian equities can be handed.

The pre-open signal is unambiguous. GIFT Nifty is quoted around 24,237, down roughly 105 points (0.43%), pointing to a gap-down start against Friday’s Nifty close near 24,175. Asia is broadly red — Kospi down about 2.4%, Nikkei and Hang Seng each down around 1–1.8% — and US futures are off about 0.3%. There is no cushion arriving from offshore.

The offsets are real but second-order. India’s Q1 FY27 GDP print lands today and consensus is strong, around 7–7.2%, against 6.8% a year earlier. Domestic institutions have been absorbing foreign supply almost exactly: FIIs sold about ₹5,040 crore of cash equities on Friday against DII buying of roughly ₹5,184 crore. And for all the daily selling, FPIs are still net buyers of about ₹30,919 crore across August — the month as a whole was an inflow month, which is a genuine counterweight to the bearish flow narrative. India VIX at 10.68 says the options market is not braced for much.

Net: sell the open, then watch 24,000. Expect weakness at the bell and a session that is really about whether the 24,000 floor holds while the GDP number and the oil tape fight each other. Technicians have the Nifty range-bound at 24,000–24,400; Bank Nifty support sits at 57,000–56,900 with resistance at 57,900–58,000. A cheap VIX into a live geopolitical event is the asymmetry worth respecting today.

What flips the call. To bullish: Hormuz de-escalates and Brent slips back under $85, GDP prints at or above 7.5%, the Nifty reclaims and holds 24,300, and the DII bid keeps fully absorbing foreign supply. To outright bearish: a clean break of 24,000, actual disruption to Hormuz traffic or Brent through $95, a GDP miss below 7%, September Fed hike odds pushing past 60%, or Bank Nifty losing 57,000. Base case in between: a gap down that gets partially bought, low conviction either way, and an Indian market taking its cue from crude and the Fed rather than from its own growth data.

The internal shape is the tell: the Nasdaq fell twice as hard as the S&P while the Dow was effectively flat, which is long-duration growth repricing against a higher rate path rather than a broad risk purge.

Friday left a constructive candle that this morning may invalidate. The Nifty printed a small bullish candle with shadows on both sides, bouncing off support near 24,000 after Thursday’s move below the ascending trendline at 24,150 failed to produce follow-through selling. Analysts read that failed breakdown as a positive setup that could open a move toward 24,300–24,400. The problem is that the setup assumed no weekend shock. A gap-down through it changes the reading.

Levels that matter today. Nifty: 24,000 is the trend-reversal line; 24,400 caps the range. Sensex remains below its 200-day EMA near 78,484, with 77,680–78,000 the zone to hold for the recovery case. Bank Nifty printed a neutral candle with a neutral RSI — 57,000–56,900 support, 57,900–58,200 resistance, and it is holding above both its 100-DMA and 200-DMA, so the structure is range-bound rather than decisively broken.

The flow picture is genuinely two-sided. Friday’s foreign selling was almost perfectly offset by domestic buying, meaning the close was domestic money absorbing supply rather than fresh demand. But zoom out and FPIs put roughly ₹30,919 crore into Indian equities across August, with flows skewing toward mid- and small-caps — attributed to the reversal of the chip trade, a stable rupee, and improving earnings growth. Both facts are true; the daily tape is heavier than the monthly one.

The India-specific vulnerability today is crude. India imports the overwhelming majority of its oil, and analysis this week put the added cost of the Hormuz crisis to India’s fossil-fuel import bill at roughly $22 billion. Expect OMCs, paints, tyres and aviation under pressure and upstream energy bid.

This is a supply-risk move, not a demand move. Oil extended gains Monday morning after the Larak Island strike, which is meaningful precisely because of what the launchers were doing: preparing to put sea mines into the Strait of Hormuz. Roughly a fifth of global seaborne crude passes through that channel. The market is not pricing an actual closure — a genuine blockade attempt would put Brent far above $90 — it is pricing a higher probability of one, plus a rising war-risk premium on shipping and insurance.

What to watch: whether Iran’s response stays confined to strikes on US positions in Jordan or extends to shipping; tanker traffic and insurance rates through Hormuz; and any OPEC+ signal on spare capacity. The level to watch on the downside is $85 — a fall back through it would say the market has decided this is another contained exchange.

Gold is the most interesting chart on the page, because it is not doing what it should. A US strike on Iran near Hormuz is a textbook safe-haven trigger, yet bullion is only a fraction firmer — and that follows a 3.2% drop on Friday as yields spiked on the hawkish Warsh read. The two forces are cancelling: geopolitics wants gold higher, a Fed that might hike wants it lower, and rates are currently winning.

Context matters here — gold rose roughly 16% over August, so a Friday give-back of that size is a well-supported asset unwinding a crowded position, not a broken trend. Indian futures were quoted around 0.85% lower in early Monday trade. Treat the spot level as approximate; gold quotes diverged materially between providers over the weekend.

Major Events to Watch (India & USA)

  • India — Q1 FY27 GDP, today. The week’s single biggest domestic number. Consensus clusters around 7–7.2% (RBI near 7%, a Reuters poll of economists at ~7.1%, Bank of Baroda 7–7.2%, SBI Research an outlier at 8%) against 6.8% in Q1 FY26. Released after market hours, so it sets Tuesday’s tone more than today’s.
  • USA — non-farm payrolls, later this week. With the Fed debate having shifted from “how many cuts” to “is the next move a hike”, a hot labour print is now the bearish outcome for risk assets. This is the week’s main global event.
  • Geopolitical — US/Iran and the Strait of Hormuz. The live risk. Escalation beyond the Jordan retaliation, or any interference with tanker traffic, is the fastest route to a repricing of everything on this page.
  • India — monthly data flow. August GST collections and auto sales land at the start of the month and will colour the domestic-demand story alongside the GDP print.
  • Fed commentary. Any walk-back — or reinforcement — of the Warsh line by other FOMC speakers moves September hike odds directly, and with them the FII flow picture for India.
