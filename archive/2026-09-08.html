Overall Call

Sideways — negative bias into the open

The tape is pointing down for a second straight session, but the reason it was pointing down is weakening. GIFT Nifty is quoted at 23,796, down 75 points, implying another soft open after Monday’s decline. Yet the single most consequential headline this morning is that Tehran says an agreement to jointly manage the Strait of Hormuz with Oman is imminent, and oil is trading lower on it. If that holds, the exact factor that has been punishing India for two weeks starts unwinding.

Monday was a straightforward risk-off day domestically. The Sensex closed at 76,132.81, down 382.62 points (−0.50%) and the Nifty 50 at 23,779.15, down 118.55 points (−0.50%). That is a fifth week of pressure and leaves the index sitting on the lower end of its recent range with 23,600 the next real support and 23,900–24,000 the ceiling desks are selling into.

Asia is mixed, not risk-on — which is a change from yesterday. Japan’s Nikkei 225 is up 0.35% and Korea’s Kospi up 1.74% on technology strength, but the Hang Seng is down 0.82%. Yesterday the regional bid was broad and India refused to follow it; today the bid is narrow and there is less to follow. Meanwhile US futures are lower — Dow futures −0.6%, S&P 500 futures −0.06% — so the reopening Wall Street session is not offering a tailwind either.

Crude at $97 is still the binding constraint, and still the swing factor. Brent has been the dominant variable for Indian equities through this drawdown: India imports the overwhelming majority of its crude, so $97 Brent is simultaneously an inflation problem, a current-account problem and a margin problem. The Hormuz de-escalation signal is the first genuine relief in that channel. It is also unconfirmed — the market is waiting on the US response, and a rejection would put crude straight back on the front foot.

Rates remain a headwind into next week. US August payrolls printed far hotter than consensus, reviving hike risk into the 15–16 September FOMC, which carries an updated Summary of Economic Projections. US CPI lands Friday 11 September. A hawkish Fed bias is a direct negative for emerging-market flows, and it is the reason gold has been sliding despite live geopolitical risk.

Domestic buyers remain the floor. On the last published session (Friday 4 September) foreign investors sold ₹3,111.94 crore of cash equities while domestic institutions bought ₹8,930.12 crore — DIIs absorbing FII selling roughly three times over. Monday’s numbers were not out at the time of writing, but this pattern is why the slide has been orderly rather than disorderly.

Net: expect a gap-down open around 23,700–23,760 into a market that is oversold, unsupported by Asia, but sitting on a genuinely improving oil headline. That is a recipe for a soft open that gets bought back rather than a trend day — unless Washington rejects the Hormuz arrangement during Indian hours.

What flips the call. To bullish: the Hormuz agreement getting a constructive US response, Brent breaking under $90, the Nifty reclaiming and holding 23,900 with breadth, or FIIs turning net buyers. To bearish: a decisive break of 23,600, Brent back through $100 on a US rejection or fresh Gulf escalation, or a soft Wall Street reopen tonight compounding into Wednesday. Confirming sideways — the base case: a gap-down that finds bids by mid-morning, crude holding $93–98 without new headlines, and the index closing inside 23,700–23,900 as desks square up ahead of Friday’s US CPI.

The pre-open signal is negative but shallow. A 75-point discount is a soft open, not a gap-down of consequence. The more useful observation is that this is the second consecutive session pointing lower, and the index has spent the last several weeks below its short- and medium-term moving averages. Markets that grind under their averages for weeks do not get the benefit of the doubt from desks — the default trade has been selling into strength near resistance.

Levels for the session. Support at 23,700, then 23,600 — a decisive break there is the bearish trigger. Resistance at 23,900, then the psychological 24,000. Given the range, an inside day is the highest-probability outcome absent a fresh Gulf headline.

Flows. Last published data (Friday 4 September): FIIs net sellers of ₹3,111.94 crore in cash, DIIs net buyers of ₹8,930.12 crore. Monday’s provisional figures were not available at the time of writing — check the NSE FII/DII report directly before acting on flow assumptions.

Crude Oil

Roughly $97 Brent, and for the first time in a fortnight the risk is two-sided. The September Brent contract is quoted at $97.06, WTI around $92.2–92.4. Feeds disagree on the intraday sign — one shows Brent down 0.25%, another shows it up over 1% at a near-identical level — so treat the direction as unresolved and the level as approximate.

The driver has changed. Through late August and early September, crude was rising on US–Iran strikes on tankers in and around the Strait of Hormuz. This morning Tehran has said an agreement to jointly manage the Strait with Oman is imminent, and oil is trading softer on it. That is the first credible de-escalation signal in this cycle.

The catch is that it is one-sided so far. The market is waiting on the US response, and nothing has been ratified. A dismissive response from Washington puts Brent back toward $100 quickly; a constructive one is the single largest potential tailwind for Indian equities in this briefing. For an economy importing the bulk of its crude, the difference between $88 and $100 Brent is the difference between a benign and a hostile macro backdrop.

Gold

Around $4,430 an ounce, and the safe-haven bid is losing to the rates story. Spot gold is quoted near $4,434, having fallen toward $4,400 on Monday and extended the prior session’s losses. Gold futures are marginally firmer this morning, up about 0.2%, with silver up 0.17%.

The interesting part is what is not happening. There is a live military confrontation affecting the world’s most important oil chokepoint, and gold is falling. That tells you rate expectations are currently the stronger force: the hot US August payrolls print pushed the market toward pricing an imminent Fed hike, which lifts real yields and the dollar — both direct headwinds for a non-yielding asset. Friday’s US CPI is the next test of that thesis.

NSE Earnings Highlights

There is no meaningful Nifty 50 earnings event today — and that is itself the point. The Q1 FY27 reporting season closed in late July and August; the market is now in the gap between results cycles, with Q2 FY27 numbers not due until October. In that vacuum, macro inputs — crude, the Fed, and flows — carry disproportionate weight in setting direction. That is a large part of why a Gulf headline is moving the Nifty more than any company-specific news.

For reference, from the season just closed: the NSE itself reported Q1 FY27 consolidated total income up 9% YoY to ₹5,252 crore and profit after tax up 7% YoY to ₹3,120 crore, with revenue from operations up 13% to ₹4,560 crore and operating EBITDA up 15% to ₹3,594 crore. Non-annualised EPS rose to ₹12.61 from ₹11.81. Those results were announced on 30 July 2026 and are relevant today mainly as context for the exchange’s own listing.

Today’s stock-specific action is being driven by news flow — Asian Paints, Swiggy, RBL Bank, Bank of Baroda and defence names — and by IPO listings, not by results.
