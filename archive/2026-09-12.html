Overall Call

Sideways — downside skew into a live Fed

The handover into Monday is genuinely mixed, which is why this is not a directional call. On the positive side, Friday’s US session was a real risk-on move and not a technical bounce: the S&P 500 rose 0.86% to 7,656.98, the Dow added 509.19 points (+0.98%) to 52,573.29 and the Nasdaq gained 0.96% to 26,333.04, all three recovering after August CPI landed close to expectations. That is the first clean up-day after a four-session slide, and it arrives with two full days of cooling-off before Indian markets reopen. India also held up better than its own briefing expected — the Nifty lost only 0.34% on a day it was set up to gap and extend.

Against that, the calendar is hostile and the commodity backdrop has not improved. The FOMC meets on Wednesday 16 September with a rate hike — not a cut — as the base case, following Chair Kevin Warsh’s Jackson Hole remarks. Market-implied odds of a 25bp hike are quoted between 56% and 90% depending on the source, which is an enormous spread and tells you the market itself has not settled. A live Fed meeting two sessions into the week is a powerful argument against adding risk on Monday, whatever Friday’s close looked like.

Crude is the structural drag and it did not go away over the weekend. Brent is around $106 and WTI in the $102–104 band, still driven by the West Asia conflict rather than by demand. For India this remains the single worst macro input available: import bill, rupee, headline inflation and downstream margins all move the wrong way together. India’s own CPI was already running at 4.45% in July, the highest since December 2024, with the RBI itself explicitly attributing pressure to energy prices and the currency.

The reason this is sideways rather than bearish is flow, and it has been the right read all week. DII buying has consistently absorbed FII selling — Thursday’s −₹438.20 crore of FII cash selling against +₹1,025.80 crore of DII buying is the pattern, not the exception. Domestic money is not flinching at $106 crude. Add that the Q1 FY27 earnings season was genuinely strong — Nifty PAT growth of 18% year-on-year, a ten-quarter high, with the upgrade-to-downgrade ratio positive at 1.5x — and the fundamental floor under this index is firmer than the macro headlines imply.

Net: expect Monday to open modestly higher on the Wall Street handover, then fade into a narrow, low-conviction range as desks refuse to position ahead of Wednesday’s Fed. Bias neutral, conviction low — deliberately low, because two consecutive bearish calls have now been beaten by domestic absorption, and because the week’s decisive information arrives on Wednesday night, not Monday morning. 23,400–23,500 is the zone to reclaim; 23,200 remains the level whose loss would restart the downtrend.

What flips the call. To bullish: the Nifty reclaiming and holding 23,500 on Monday with follow-through from Bank Nifty; Brent breaking back below $100 on any West Asia de-escalation; or Fed hike odds collapsing below 40% on soft data or dovish commentary before Wednesday. To bearish: a sustained break of 23,200; Brent pushing through $110 on a confirmed physical supply interruption rather than a risk premium; FII cash selling accelerating past roughly ₹3,000 crore a day, which would mean DII absorption is no longer sufficient; or the Fed hiking and signalling more to come. Confirming the base case: a small gap up that fades, thin volumes, and an index that spends Monday and Tuesday inside a 200-point range waiting for Wednesday.

Crude Oil

Brent is around $106 and WTI in the $102–104 band — still triple-digit, but no longer accelerating. Brent prints between $105.82 and $106.11, and one feed shows it down 1.41% on the day. WTI is quoted between $102.59 and $104.02 across sources, a spread wide enough that the band rather than a point estimate is the honest figure. The important change from yesterday is not the level but the direction: after a week of relentless upward pressure, Friday saw Brent give a little back. One down day is not a trend, but it is the first crack in a one-way move.

The driver is unchanged and it is geopolitical, not fundamental. Prices are elevated on escalating West Asia tensions affecting global energy supply — a risk premium built on the possibility of disruption rather than on any confirmed physical outage. That distinction remains the whole trade. A risk premium can unwind in a single headline; a real supply interruption cannot. Nothing sourced this week confirms an actual outage.

For India the arithmetic does not change over a weekend. Crude above $100 pressures the import bill, the rupee, headline CPI and the margins of everything downstream. It is already visible in the earnings data: oil marketing companies were the single drag on an otherwise strong Q1 FY27, with elevated crude weighing directly on their results. If Brent’s Friday softness extends into next week, that is the most plausible source of a broad Indian relief rally — more so than anything the Fed does.

Gold

Gold is holding around $4,370–4,385 an ounce, near a one-month low, and it is doing something unusual. Feeds print $4,385.61 (up a marginal $2.61, +0.06%) and $4,361.34 later in the session — call it approximate. What is notable is that gold opened Friday at its lowest level in over a month despite an active geopolitical conflict and $106 crude. That is not what safe-haven demand alone would produce.

The explanation is the Fed, and it is the cleanest cross-current on this page. With a rate hike priced for 16 September and hike odds quoted as high as 90% after the CPI print, the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset is rising. Higher real rates are a direct headwind for gold, and right now that headwind is roughly cancelling the geopolitical bid. The metal is caught between two forces of similar size, which is exactly why it is flat near a one-month low rather than making new highs in a war.

Read-through for Indian investors. Gold failing to rally on genuine geopolitical stress is a signal worth taking seriously: it says the market is currently more worried about monetary tightening than about supply shocks. If the Fed hikes on Wednesday and signals more, gold likely stays capped. If it holds, or hikes and sounds finished, the geopolitical bid should reassert. Note also that the rupee-denominated move will outrun the dollar move if the currency softens on the oil bill, which cushions local holders either way. One source reports a January 2026 peak near $5,597, which would put the current level well off its high — that figure is single-sourced and should be verified before being relied on.

Major Events to Watch (India & USA)

FOMC, Wednesday 16 September. This is the week. The Federal Reserve meets with a 25 basis point rate hike as the base case — a hike, in a year that began with cuts priced. The shift followed Chair Kevin Warsh’s Jackson Hole keynote, and August’s CPI hardened it further. Market-implied odds are quoted anywhere from 56% to 90% across sources, and that spread is itself the story: this is a genuinely live meeting, not a formality. For Indian equities the transmission is via the dollar, US yields and emerging-market risk appetite, all of which tighten on a hawkish outcome.

  • West Asia conflict (ongoing). Still the variable with the widest distribution of outcomes. De-escalation takes Brent below $100 and is the fastest available route to an Indian relief rally; a confirmed physical supply interruption takes it to $110 and overwhelms everything else on this page.
  • US Treasury yields. Yields have been an independent driver of the sell-off, not merely a follower of oil. Watch whether Friday’s equity relief was matched by a yield retreat or whether stocks rallied while bonds stayed pressured — the second would make the rally fragile. This could not be confirmed inside the window.
  • India CPI and IIP (India). August retail inflation and industrial production are due around mid-September. July CPI ran at 4.45%, the highest since December 2024, with the RBI attributing the pressure to energy prices and a softer rupee; food and beverages at 5.52% and transport at 4.43% were the pressure points. The RBI’s own projection sits near 4.2% against a 4% target and a ±2% band. An August print pushing toward 5% would revive the question of whether the RBI’s room to support growth is closing. The exact release date could not be confirmed.
  • The rupee (India). Not a scheduled event, but the channel through which $106 crude reaches domestic inflation. A weekly watch item for as long as Brent stays above $100.
  • Monday’s Asian open. The first market anywhere to price Friday’s US rally, and the best available preview of the Indian open. Worth checking before 09:15 IST.

NSE Earnings Highlights

There are no results today and none scheduled Monday — the calendar is between cycles. Q1 FY27 reporting is complete and the Q2 FY27 season does not begin in earnest until the second half of October, when the IT majors traditionally open proceedings. No index-weight Nifty 50 result is expected in the coming week.

The Q1 FY27 season that just finished was the strongest in over two years, and it deserves more weight than it is getting. Nifty companies delivered 18% year-on-year PAT growth — a ten-quarter high — with the strength broad-based across 19 sectors exceeding expectations and the earnings upgrade-to-downgrade ratio turning positive at 1.5x. Growth leaders were financials, metals, oil & gas excluding OMCs, technology and telecom, with automobiles, chemicals, textiles and real estate also strong. FY27 Nifty EPS estimates were nudged up 0.6% to ₹1,232 and FY28 up 0.3% to ₹1,425.

The one soft spot is the one the crude story predicts. OMCs were the drag on aggregate earnings, with elevated oil prices weighing directly on their results. That is the whole macro thesis showing up in a P&L line: as long as Brent holds above $100, the downstream complex keeps subtracting from index earnings while upstream and financials carry it. Anyone positioning around the crude view should be looking at that split rather than at the index.

One source reports Nifty-50 net profit growth of 9.8% for the same quarter against the 18% figure cited above — the difference is almost certainly index-aggregate versus broader-universe methodology, but it was not resolved inside the research window. Both figures are directionally positive.
