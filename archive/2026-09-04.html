Overall Call

Sideways, upward tilt

The rate scare that drove this week’s selling has eased, but nothing has been resolved. Fed Governor Christopher Waller said he would be comfortable holding rates where they are provided price pressure does not surprise to the upside — a direct counterweight to Chair Kevin Warsh’s hawkish Jackson Hole framing. Asia read that as relief and rallied. That is the whole of this morning’s good news, and it is a repricing of expectations, not a change in facts.

The tape is constructive going in. Wall Street closed higher for a second straight session on Thursday, with all three majors up around half a percent as Treasury yields retreated. Nikkei +0.7–0.8%, Kospi +1.0–1.1%, Chinese blue-chips +1%. GIFT Nifty at 24,019, up 52 points, signals a positive Indian open.

Two weights argue against chasing it. First, crude is in the mid-$90s after US strikes on Iranian tankers, with Hormuz transits down from 23 to 10 in a week. For an importer of roughly 85% of its crude, that is a live current-account and margin problem regardless of what the Fed does. Second, the rupee is at a record low near 95.74/USD — the currency is already telling the oil story.

The structural reason to expect a range: the day’s biggest event lands after Indian cash closes. US August payrolls print at 18:00 IST, two and a half hours after the 15:30 bell. Consensus is +56,000 after July’s shock −23,000, unemployment steady at 4.1%, wages +0.2% m/m. Nobody carries size into a print they cannot trade until Monday. Expect a firm open, then drift.

What flips the call. To bullish: Brent breaking back under $88 on Hormuz de-escalation, the rupee recovering off its record low, FIIs extending the buying seen on 2 September, and the Nifty taking and holding 24,000–24,100 with breadth. To bearish: Brent decisively through $100, a fresh escalation headline out of the Gulf during Indian hours, the rupee sliding past 96, hike odds for the 16 September FOMC re-firming above 70%, or a sustained Nifty break of 23,800. Confirming sideways: crude holding $93–96 without new headlines and the index grinding in roughly 23,880–24,050 on falling volume into the payrolls print — the base case.

Thursday split the index from the market. The Sensex fell 0.55% while the Nifty gave up only 0.17% — and beneath both, Nifty Midcap 100 rose 0.37% and Smallcap 100 rose 1.20%. Private banks and realty outperformed; Nifty Realty and Media led the gainers. Heavyweight drag, healthy breadth: that is a market rotating, not one being sold.

Flows were supportive going into the week. On 2 September FIIs were strong net buyers at ₹6,688 crore with DIIs adding ₹2,813 crore. The 3 September figures were not confirmed before this went out — treat the flow picture as one day stale.

The rupee is the constraint to watch. At a record low near 95.74, pressured by the oil bill, portfolio outflows and West Asia risk, with the RBI intervening to smooth volatility. A weak currency in an oil shock caps how far foreign money will extend, however good the domestic breadth looks.

Crude Oil

Brent is in the mid-$90s, WTI near $93. Quotes ranged from $95.25 to $96.24 on Brent across feeds this morning — approximate, but the level is not in doubt.

The driver is military, not economic. US forces reportedly struck two Iranian government tankers at anchor on 2 September — the first time tankers themselves were targeted in retaliation for Iranian attacks on shipping rather than to enforce the blockade — as part of a package reported to hit roughly 100 IRGC-linked targets. Prices surged past $95 on the news, extending a 4.6% rally.

The number that matters most is transit volume. Hormuz crossings fell from 23 the previous Wednesday to 10 on Monday. That is the physical supply channel narrowing in real time, and it is why this is not fading like a headline-driven spike would.

Gold

Spot gold around $4,374 — a single-feed read this morning, so approximate. Recent sessions have seen it pressured by building Fed rate-hike expectations, then supported when those expectations soften.

Gold is caught between its two drivers and they are pulling opposite ways. Safe-haven demand from the Gulf escalation argues up; a Fed that might hike — raising the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset — argues down. Waller’s comments tilted the rate side friendlier overnight. Tonight’s payrolls print resolves more of this than anything else on the calendar: a strong number revives the hike trade and pressures gold; a weak one does the reverse.

Major Events to Watch (India & USA)

  • US August non-farm payrolls — today, 18:00 IST (08:30 ET). The single most important item. Consensus roughly +56,000, unemployment 4.1%, average hourly earnings +0.2% m/m, against July’s −23,000. Lands after Indian cash closes; the reaction is a Monday event here.
  • FOMC, 15–16 September. Rates have been at 3.50–3.75% since December 2025. The live question is a hike. Odds had built toward 66–70% after Chair Warsh’s Jackson Hole remarks and have cooled on Waller’s comments — the exact current probability was not verified this morning.
  • US–Iran / Strait of Hormuz. The dominant tail risk in both directions. Any new strike or, conversely, any credible de-escalation moves crude, the rupee and Indian sentiment inside the session.
  • RBI. Policy rate held at 5.25% for a fifth consecutive meeting, with headline inflation above target and core described as moderate. No scheduled decision today; watch for intervention as the rupee sets new lows.
  • India macro backdrop. GST collections hit a record ₹2.11 lakh crore in July, up 15.4% y/y with import-linked GST up 28.8% — a genuinely strong domestic signal running against the external squeeze.
