Overall Call

Sideways — cautious, with a soft open

The single most useful fact this morning is a divergence. Asia is having an excellent session — Japan’s Nikkei 225 is up 2.23% and Korea’s Kospi up 3.04% in early deals, led by technology. Yet GIFT Nifty is quoted at 23,968, down 81 points, and against Nifty futures near 24,048 that implies a gap-down open. When regional risk appetite is that strong and India still points lower, the drag is India-specific. It is oil.

Brent near $97 is the dominant variable. September ICE futures were quoted at $96.73, up 0.89%, after the US and Iran exchanged strikes on each other’s tankers in the Strait of Hormuz over the weekend. Brent is up roughly 9% on the week and close to 16% on the month. For an economy that imports the overwhelming majority of its crude, that is simultaneously an inflation problem, a current-account problem and a margin problem — and it is the clearest channel by which a Gulf headline lands on Indian earnings. This is why India cannot simply import Asia’s tech rally.

The domestic tape was already weak going in. The Nifty closed Friday at 23,897.70, up a token 24 points (+0.10%), and the Sensex at 76,515.43, up 362.57 (+0.48%). That snapped a losing streak but changed nothing structurally: on the week the Nifty fell about 1.15%, a fourth consecutive weekly decline, closing below both its 21-day and 55-day EMAs. A market four weeks down and under both averages does not get the benefit of the doubt, and desks are running sell-on-rise near resistance.

Rates still point the wrong way, but less violently than Friday suggested. US August payrolls printed at +162,000 against a consensus near 53,000, which pushed Treasury yields up and all three US majors down on revived hike risk into the 15–16 September FOMC (current target range 3.50–3.75%). Asia’s overnight strength suggests some of that hawkish repricing has already been walked back. Either way, a US hiking bias is a straightforward negative for emerging-market flows.

The offset is domestic and it is real. On Friday foreign investors sold ₹3,111.94 crore of cash equities while domestic institutions bought ₹8,930.12 crore — DIIs absorbed FII selling nearly three times over. That is the pattern that has kept this four-week slide orderly rather than disorderly, and it is the main reason to expect a range today rather than a break.

Net: a soft open around 23,850–23,900, a market with no US cue to chase, strong regional tailwinds fighting an oil-driven domestic headwind, and domestic buyers under the tape. That is a recipe for chop, not a trend.

What flips the call. To bullish: the Nifty reclaiming and holding 24,050 with breadth behind it, Asia’s tech strength spilling into Indian IT during the session, Brent slipping under $88 on any Hormuz de-escalation, or FIIs turning net buyers. To bearish: a decisive break of 23,800 (which opens 23,600), Brent through $100, or a fresh Gulf escalation headline landing during Indian hours. Confirming sideways — the base case: a gap-down that gets bought, crude holding $93–98 without new headlines, and volume fading through the afternoon into a shut US market.

Global Markets

Asia is the story this morning, and it is a strong one. Japan and Korea are both sharply higher in early deals on technology strength, extending Friday’s regional bounce. That is notable because it comes despite a lower Wall Street close, which implies the hawkish payrolls reaction is being faded rather than compounded.

The US closed lower on Friday and does not trade today. The payrolls beat was read as a policy threat rather than a growth confirmation — yields jumped, equities fell, and the decline was orderly rather than violent. With Labor Day closing US markets, Friday’s levels stand until Tuesday.

Europe is a look-ahead. The FTSE 100 and DAX have not opened. They will take their cue from Asia’s tech bid on one side and crude’s inflation signal on the other, with no US session to follow them into the afternoon.

Structure. Four straight losing weeks, a close below both the 21-day and 55-day EMAs, and rebounds that keep getting sold. Until that changes, rallies are levels to fade rather than trends to join.

Levels that matter. Nifty support at 23,800, and a decisive break there opens 23,600. Resistance at 24,050, with 24,250 the more serious hurdle. Sensex support around 76,079 and 75,808; resistance 76,952 and 77,223.

Flows. The FII/DII split is doing the heavy lifting. Foreign selling has been persistent through the slide; domestic institutions have absorbed it comfortably. That asymmetry is the difference between a grinding four-week drift and a proper correction.

Currency. The rupee firmed roughly 1% last week, helped by the RBI’s special FCNR(B) swap window. A firmer rupee takes some of the sting out of $97 Brent and removes the currency-panic tail that usually rides along with an oil spike.

Primary market is busy today. Pranav Constructions (₹351.03 crore) and Apana Logistics (₹34.14 crore) open for subscription; Qualiance International enters day two. This is a geopolitical bid, not a demand bid. The US and Iran exchanged strikes on each other’s tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, with Washington targeting Iranian oil tankers and Tehran hitting tankers and firing a ballistic missile toward US warships near the Strait. Traffic through Hormuz has been disrupted on and off for much of the past five months.

Why the number is large. Roughly a quarter of seaborne crude and petroleum products and about a fifth of LNG moved through the Strait before the crisis. A risk premium on that chokepoint is not a supply/demand rebalancing story that fades on inventory data — it fades on de-escalation headlines, which arrive without warning in either direction.

For India specifically: pressure on OMC marketing margins, on aviation and paints and chemicals input costs, on the import bill, and on the CPI print due Saturday. It is the reason today’s call is cautious despite a strong Asian tape.
