Overall Call

Sideways — bearish tilt into Monday

The call has hardened since yesterday, and the reason is the Fed. Firmer reporting on Kevin Warsh’s Jackson Hole debut shows the market did not merely read him as “leaning hawkish” — it repriced. Odds of a September rate increase moved from roughly one-in-three before the speech to better than 50/50 after it, and the CME FedWatch tool now carries around a four-in-ten chance of a quarter-point hike by December. J.P. Morgan Global Research expects the first hike in December. The backdrop is July PCE inflation at 3.7% year-on-year, core at 3.3%. This is no longer a market debating the pace of cuts; it is a market pricing hikes.

That matters more to India than to Wall Street. A rising US rate path is the single most reliable driver of foreign outflows from emerging markets, and the flow data already shows it happening. FIIs sold roughly ₹5,040 crore of Indian cash equities on Friday against DII buying of about ₹5,184 crore — a near-exact offset, meaning Friday’s green close was domestic money absorbing foreign supply rather than fresh demand arriving. Alongside that, FIIs are reported carrying a net short index-futures book of roughly 2,02,633 contracts. Cash selling plus a large short book is a coherent, funded bearish view from the market’s marginal price-setter.

The counterweight is domestic and it is real. India’s Q1 FY27 GDP print lands Monday, 31 August, and expectations are strong: RBI has projected around 7%, a Reuters poll of 58 economists sits near 7.1%, Bank of Baroda models 7–7.2%, and SBI Research is the outlier at 8%. Against Q1 FY26’s 6.8%, any print in that band is an acceleration. India VIX at 10.68 says the options market expects nothing dramatic — which is itself the risk, because a big surprise in either direction is not paid for.

Net: a market pinned between a strong domestic number and a deteriorating global rate backdrop. Expect a rangebound Monday around 24,000–24,300 with the GDP print setting the intraday tone and the FII bid — or its continued absence — setting the close.

What flips the call. To bullish: GDP prints at or above 7.5%, the Nifty takes out 24,300 and holds it, FII futures shorts begin covering, and Fed speakers walk the hawkish tone back. To bearish: a break of 24,000, a GDP miss below 7%, another week of ₹5,000-crore-a-day foreign selling that the DII bid stops absorbing, September hike odds pushing decisively past 60%, or Brent through $90. Base case in between: low-conviction chop, with India reacting to US rate news rather than to its own growth data.

But it is doing so against an FII position that is short in both cash and futures, and the Fed repricing described above gives foreign desks a fresh reason to stay that way. The practical read is unchanged in shape but sharper in degree: persistent foreign supply caps the upside, domestic inflows cushion the downside, and the market grinds in a band until one side gives.

India VIX at 10.68 is the underappreciated detail. Volatility that low, on the eve of a GDP release and with a Fed that may hike in three weeks, means protection is cheap because nobody expects to need it. That is a reasonable base case and a poor place to be wrong from.

Crude is firming slowly, and that is the wrong direction for India. Brent around $88.3 and WTI around $83.4 are quiet levels in isolation, but the trend over recent sessions has been a grind higher off the lows — Brent was being quoted near $87 mid-week. For an economy importing the large majority of its crude, a tailwind is quietly turning into a neutral.

It is not yet a headwind. $88 Brent does not by itself change the inflation arithmetic or the rupee story. The number that would is $90. A sustained move through it starts showing up in oil-marketing-company margins, in aviation costs, and in current-account commentary — and turns a footnote into a sector call.

Gold

Gold is quoting near $4,460 an ounce, and that requires an explanation rather than a headline. Two independent feeds checked this morning — one printing $4,467.20 late Saturday, another $4,454.40 on the Kitco bid — agree within about 0.3% of each other. They do not agree with Saturday’s edition of this briefing, which reported gold above $4,600 on the same weekend. Bullion does not trade on Saturdays, so one of those readings is wrong. This page goes with the twice-confirmed lower figure and treats the level as approximate.

The mechanism to watch is straightforward. Gold pays no interest, so it competes directly with cash and bonds. A Fed that is now more likely than not to raise rates in September raises the opportunity cost of holding it — which is a genuine headwind, and consistent with a price nearer $4,450 than $4,600. Against that, hedging demand tends to rise when a central bank is fighting 3.7% inflation. Those two forces are currently pulling in opposite directions, and the honest position is that the near-term direction is unresolved.

For context, the 2026 record high of roughly $5,602 was set on 28 January. Current levels sit well below it.

Major Events to Watch (India & USA)

India — Monday is a real event day.

  • Q1 FY27 GDP, released Monday 31 August. The single most important scheduled item in this briefing. Consensus clusters at 7–7.2% (Bank of Baroda), a Reuters poll of 58 economists sits near 7.1%, RBI has projected around 7%, and SBI Research is the bull at 8%. Q1 FY26 was 6.8%. Momentum is attributed to manufacturing, electricity and construction, supported by government capex. A print at 7.5%+ is a genuine upside surprise; below 7% is a miss the market has not priced.
  • The month-start data cluster — manufacturing PMI, GST collections, auto sales — typically lands 1–2 September. Exact dates were not confirmed against a primary calendar this run; treat as the usual pattern, not a verified schedule.

  • Brent through $90 remains the cleanest single trigger for a macro re-rating of the Indian market.

  • The early-September US data cluster — PMI, ISM, and the labour-market prints — will be read entirely through the rate lens, with strong data now bearish for equities rather than bullish.
