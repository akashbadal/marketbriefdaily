Overall Call

Sideways — with a cautious tilt into Monday

Friday looked better on the tape than it did underneath it. The Sensex snapped a two-day losing streak, rising 330.92 points (+0.43%) to 77,264.51, and the Nifty added 84.80 points (+0.35%) to 24,175.65, with TCS, Tech Mahindra and Infosys leading. That is a perfectly respectable close. But the flow data underneath it points the other way: FIIs sold roughly ₹5,040 crore in the cash segment while DIIs bought about ₹5,184 crore — a near-exact offset. Friday’s gain was domestic money absorbing foreign supply, not new buying arriving.

The positioning is the more serious signal. FIIs are reported carrying a net short index-futures book of roughly 2,02,633 contracts. That is a large, directional, and unambiguously bearish stance from the marginal price-setter in this market. It cuts two ways: it caps rallies while it persists, but it is also fuel — a decisive upside break would force covering. For now, treat it as a lid.

The global handover into Monday is mildly negative but not damaging. Wall Street closed Friday lower after Fed Chair Kevin Warsh’s first Jackson Hole keynote was read as leaning hawkish on inflation: the S&P 500 fell 0.25% to 7,711.76, the Nasdaq 0.52% to 26,402.42, and the Dow was essentially unchanged, off 9.45 points to 53,559.99. Note the shape — growth names took the harder hit, which is the direct opposite of what carried Indian IT on Friday. All three US indices still finished the week higher, so this is a wobble, not a turn.

Commodities add a small drag. Brent at roughly $88 is up from the $87 area quoted mid-week; for an import-dependent economy that is a marginal negative on the margin, not yet a macro problem. Gold holding above $4,600 near a three-month high says money is still paying up for a hedge, which is not the tell you want alongside a hawkish central banker.

What flips the call. To bullish: the Nifty opens firm and takes out 24,300 on IT follow-through, FII futures shorts start covering, and crude slips back under $86 — that reopens 24,500. To bearish: a break of 24,000, a second week of ₹5,000-crore-a-day FII selling that the DII bid stops absorbing, a hawkish follow-up from Fed speakers, or Brent pressing through $90. The base case in between: a rangebound, low-conviction Monday in the 24,000–24,300 band, with the market waiting for US data later in the week rather than leading it.

The streak-snapping matters less than who did the buying. The Sensex ended a two-session slide, and IT did the heavy lifting — TCS, Tech Mahindra and Infosys were the top Nifty gainers, the delayed read-through from Thursday’s enterprise-software strength on Wall Street finally landing. Beneath the headline, breadth was unremarkable: the Nifty MidCap rose about 0.05% and the Nifty SmallCap about 0.2%, both lagging the large-cap benchmark. This was a narrow, index-led day.

The flow picture is the one to carry into Monday. Foreign institutions sold roughly ₹5,040 crore of cash equities on Friday against domestic buying of about ₹5,184 crore. The domestic bid has been the load-bearing wall of this market for weeks and it held again. But an FII net short of some 2,02,633 index-futures contracts alongside that cash selling is a coherent bearish position, not noise — foreign desks are positioned for the Nifty to go down, and are paying to hold that view.

The practical read: persistent FII supply caps upside, robust DII inflows cushion downside, and the result is a market that grinds sideways in a band until one side gives. That is exactly what the last several sessions have looked like, and nothing in Friday’s data breaks the pattern.

Both benchmarks eased on Friday, but the weekly direction is the wrong one for India. Brent slipping 0.34% to about $88.22 and WTI 0.86% to about $82.82 is a quiet day in isolation. The context is that Brent was being quoted around $87 mid-week after an extended run of declines — so the trend of the last several sessions has been a slow firming off the lows, not a continuation lower.

For an economy importing the large majority of its crude, that reverses a genuine tailwind. It is not yet a headwind: $88 Brent is not a level that changes the inflation arithmetic or the rupee story on its own. The number to watch is $90. A move through it would start showing up in oil-marketing-company margins, in aviation, and in the current-account commentary — and would turn a footnote into a sector call.

Gold

Gold is doing the thing that should make equity bulls slightly uncomfortable. Spot held above $4,600 an ounce through Friday, defending a climb toward a three-month high, with buyers absorbing profit-taking as the dollar drifted lower into the Warsh keynote. Feeds disagreed on the exact print — quotes across providers ran from roughly $4,599 to $4,632 — so read the level as approximate and the behaviour as the signal.

The mechanism is straightforward and worth stating plainly. Gold pays no interest, so it competes directly with cash and bonds. If the Fed holds in September, the opportunity cost of owning it falls and it has room to run. If Warsh’s hawkish tone hardens into an actual hike path, that reverses. The fact that gold rose into a hawkish speech rather than falling suggests the bid is coming from hedging demand rather than rate expectations — investors buying protection they expect to need.

Near-term forecasts published Friday put the 31 August range at roughly $4,577 to $4,698. That is a wide band and should be treated as a market-consensus sketch, not a target.

NSE Earnings Highlights

The Q1 FY27 season is effectively over, and that itself is the point. The statutory filing deadline for the June quarter passed on 14 August, and by end-July more than 761 companies had already reported. There is no meaningful large-cap Nifty 50 earnings catalyst scheduled for the coming week — the calendar is empty, which means macro and flows have the floor to themselves. That is part of why the market has been trading on FII positioning rather than fundamentals.

What the completed season said. The dominant theme was strong sales growth paired with weak profit growth — revenue held up across the economy while margins compressed under cost pressure. The gap between the two lines is the story of the quarter.

The split by sector was clean. Companies levered to domestic investment — manufacturing, infrastructure, financial services — outperformed. Export-oriented and consumer-facing names continued to struggle. Note the tension with Friday’s tape: IT led the index on a global software read-through, but the domestic earnings evidence has been pointing at domestic-facing capex plays, not exporters. One of those two signals is wrong.
