Asian tech cracks — Kospi −8% — and GIFT Nifty barely blinks

Tuesday, 28 July 2026 · Prepared before the 09:15 IST open · US levels are Monday's close; Asian levels are live

Written at ~08:40 IST. Wall Street closed hours ago and those numbers are final. Asian markets are one to two hours into their session, so those moves will drift materially before India opens. Europe has not opened. Research window was compressed today — gaps are flagged in the text.

Overall Call

Sideways with a downside tilt

The dominant fact this morning is a violent unwind in Asian technology that India is, so far, refusing to import. South Korea's Kospi is down 8.04% with SK Hynix off roughly 10%; Japan's Nikkei 225 is down 3.84%. That is a memory/AI-capex de-rating ahead of Amazon, Meta and Apple earnings this week, not a macro shock. And yet GIFT Nifty is quoted at 24,005, down just 23 points — essentially flat. The market is signalling that this is a semiconductor problem, and the Nifty carries very little semiconductor weight.

India also has a genuine tailwind. Crude keeps falling on US–Iran diplomacy — ICE futures around $87.50, down about 1% and now below $88, having been near $100 a week ago. For a large net oil importer that is a direct positive for the current account, inflation, OMCs, paints, aviation and tyres. Nifty snapped a five-session losing streak yesterday with a 1% gain to 23,995.95, so it enters today with momentum rather than damage.

Why not simply bullish. Three restraints. First, a Kospi down 8% does not stay contained for a full session very often; if global risk desks start cutting exposure broadly, India's IT pack and FII flows get hit second-hand. Second, it is monthly F&O expiry week, so positioning noise will exaggerate any move in either direction. Third, the Fed decides tomorrow, and pricing has drifted hawkish — roughly a 30% chance of a hike this week and about 80% by September on the readings available this morning — which is a poor backdrop for adding risk. FIIs were still net sellers yesterday (−₹1,688 cr) against DII buying of ₹2,329 cr.

Net: expect a flat-to-slightly-lower open, a defensive tilt away from IT and toward oil-linked and domestic consumption names, and a session that is decided by whether Asia stabilises through the morning. Respect 24,000 as the pivot.

What flips the call: turns bullish if Asia bases out by mid-morning, Nifty holds above 24,000 and closes over 24,100–24,170, Brent stays under $88, and HUL and L&T deliver clean numbers. Turns bearish if the Kospi/Nikkei damage broadens beyond semis, Nifty loses Monday's gap zone at 23,823–23,891 — which opens 23,600 — or if the Fed reads hawkish tomorrow, or if the Iran talks stall and crude snaps back above $95.

US (settled). Monday was the same split tape that has defined the past week. The Dow rose 0.51% to 52,210.08 on the oil retreat, the S&P 500 essentially unchanged at 7,413.18, and the Nasdaq slipped 0.18% to 24,932.08 as chip and memory names stayed under pressure — Sandisk fell sharply on memory weakness and Nvidia weighed on the index intraday. Falling crude is helping the industrial half of the market while the AI-capex question keeps taking value out of the technology half.

Asia (in progress, and the story of the morning). The Asia-Pacific region is broadly red as investors unwind technology bets ahead of Amazon, Meta and Apple earnings. Korea's Kospi is down 8.04% — an outsized single-session move driven by SK Hynix falling about 10% — and Japan's Nikkei 225 is down 3.84%. Australia's ASX 200 is off just 0.23%, which tells you how concentrated this is in semiconductors rather than broad risk appetite. US futures are soft in sympathy, with Dow and S&P futures down 0.12% and 0.25%. We were unable to confirm live Hang Seng and Shanghai levels within today's research window — treat Greater China as unread rather than assumed flat.

Europe (look-ahead). Not yet open. Europe carries far less semiconductor weight than North Asia and benefits directly from cheaper crude, so the FTSE 100 and DAX should open better than the Asian tape implies — though ASML and the semi-equipment complex will track the Korean move closely.

The pre-open signal. GIFT Nifty at 24,005, down 23 points, is the cleanest live read at this hour and it points to a marginally lower start. What matters is not the 23 points but the contrast: Korea is down 8% and India is being marked down by 0.1%. That divergence is the single most useful piece of information on this page. It will either be validated during the session or closed violently.

What changed yesterday. Monday ended a five-session losing streak with a 1% gain, Nifty +228.50 to 23,995.95 and Sensex +776.01 to 76,835.78. Three drivers: easing West Asia tensions, the sharp fall in crude, and positioning ahead of the Fed. Oil marketing companies were visible beneficiaries, with HPCL, BPCL and IOC up around 3%. IDFC First Bank jumped about 9% to a 52-week high after Q1 profit more than doubled.

Levels. The Nifty formed a bullish candle with a higher high and higher low. As long as it holds above Monday's gap zone of 23,823–23,891, the pullback stays intact and 24,100 then 24,170 are the near-term targets. Lose that gap and the move reverts to the downtrend, with 23,600 the next reference. 24,000 is the psychological pivot on the open. Volatility should be elevated into monthly F&O expiry.

Flows. FIIs sold ₹1,688.23 cr in the cash market on Monday against DII buying of ₹2,329.14 cr — a smaller foreign sale than the prior session, again absorbed domestically. Note that FII/DII figures are provisional at this hour and are commonly revised.

The war premium is still draining out. Prices fell again on optimism over a diplomatic resolution between Washington and Tehran. President Trump said talks with Iran were going well but that the US would resume strikes if negotiations stalled; Iran struck a similar tone. ICE futures were quoted at $87.52, down 0.95%. That is roughly $12 below where Brent traded a week ago.

Treat the exact levels as approximate. Providers are materially out of step this morning — one widely-followed data service showed crude at $82.62 on Monday after an 8.68% single-day fall, while the futures quote above is $87.52. Part of that is Brent-versus-WTI and part is contract and timestamp differences, but the honest position is that the precise print is uncertain while the direction is not.

The tail risk has not gone. Yemen's Houthi movement is reported to be seeking to replicate Iran's Hormuz strategy in the Red Sea by disrupting shipping through Bab el-Mandeb. A second chokepoint reopening as a threat is exactly what would put the premium back.

Gold is caught between two forces and is resolving sideways. Spot traded around $4,090 an ounce late Monday US time, up roughly 1% on the day. The safe-haven bid faded as the Iran situation de-escalated, but the softer dollar that came with lower oil supported the metal. This morning gold and silver futures are both giving some of that back, down 0.67% and 1.79%.

The Fed is the swing factor. With markets attaching a meaningful probability to a hike — roughly 30% this week and around 80% by September on the readings available this morning — the rate path is now the binding constraint on gold, not geopolitics. The expected range into today is broadly $4,008–$4,157. A hawkish Fed tomorrow pressures the metal; any dovish surprise, or a re-escalation in the Gulf, takes it back toward the top of that band.

Major Events to Watch (India & USA)

  • US Federal Reserve decision — Wednesday 29 July. The single biggest scheduled event of the week. A hold is the base case, but the hawkish drift in pricing means the statement and press conference matter more than the decision.
  • Megacap US earnings this week — Amazon, Meta, Apple. These are the direct cause of the Asian tech unwind. Guidance on AI capital expenditure will set the tone for global technology, and by extension Indian IT.
  • Monthly F&O expiry in India. Rollover activity will amplify intraday moves independently of news. Expect elevated volatility and treat single-session index moves with some scepticism this week.
  • US–Iran negotiations. Live and unresolved. Trump has explicitly said strikes resume if talks stall. This is the main two-way risk for crude, and therefore for Indian inflation and the rupee.
  • Red Sea / Bab el-Mandeb. Houthi statements about disrupting shipping are a slower-burning but real threat to freight rates and energy transit.
