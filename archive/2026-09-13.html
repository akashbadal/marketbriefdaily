Overall Call

Sideways — downside skew into a live Fed

The call is unchanged from yesterday, and the four-day holiday gap is the reason it stays non-directional. India reopens on Tuesday 15 September having missed Monday’s and part of Tuesday’s global tape, and the single largest catalyst of the week — the FOMC decision on Wednesday 16 September, with a hike near-fully priced by some feeds — lands after Tuesday’s Indian close. Tuesday is therefore a catch-up and positioning session, not a resolution session. That argues against a large directional move in either direction and against putting much weight on Tuesday’s print as a signal.

The skew is downward for four specific reasons. First, the Nifty has fallen five weeks running, closing the week at 23,398.10, down 2.09% week-on-week — this is an index with no recent demonstrated ability to hold a bounce. Second, crude is sitting around $105 Brent with WTI near or above $100, which is a direct terms-of-trade tax on an oil-importing economy and feeds straight into the rupee and into imported inflation. Third, FIIs were net sellers in the most recent confirmable session, with DII buying doing the absorbing — a flow structure that holds an index up but rarely lifts it. Fourth, India VIX rose 4.15% on the week, so the market is already paying up for protection ahead of the event.

The counterweight is real and should not be dismissed. Wall Street closed the week with a firm rebound of roughly 1% across the major indices after August CPI came in close enough to forecast to relieve the worst fears, and GIFT Nifty was last seen around 20 points higher before the break — a mildly positive, and now stale, handover. Financial services held the Indian market together through last week’s selling while IT, metals, realty and autos did the damage; a market whose largest weight is stable is a market that grinds rather than breaks. This is the third consecutive edition to note that this tape absorbs bad news better than the crude-and-Fed narrative alone would predict, and that observation has been the correct one twice running.

What flips the call bullish: the Fed holds on Wednesday rather than hiking, or hikes with language that clearly signals it is the last one; Brent breaks and holds below roughly $100, taking WTI into the low nineties; FIIs turn net buyers for two or more consecutive sessions; the Nifty reclaims and holds above roughly 23,700–23,800, which would end the five-week losing sequence and confirm the sellers are done.

What flips the call bearish: the Fed hikes and the dot plot points to more, pushing the US 10-year decisively through 5%; crude breaks higher on a Strait of Hormuz escalation after the Oman talks fail; FII selling accelerates past the recent run-rate; the Nifty loses 23,200 on a closing basis, which opens the door toward 23,000 and turns a correction into something that needs a new floor.

Note on process: yesterday’s edition owned that its bearish calls had been directionally early twice and wrong on the catalyst once. That record is the reason this page is deliberately refusing a directional call into an event it cannot see the other side of.

Global Markets

Wall Street ended the week on the front foot. The rebound snapped a four-day slide that had been the S&P’s deepest since June, driven by crude punching above $100 and the US 10-year yield closing in on 5%. August CPI was the trigger for the relief: headline rose 0.4% month-on-month, in line with forecasts (up sharply from July’s 0.1%), while core came in at 0.3% against a 0.2% consensus. That split — headline matched, core ran a tenth hot — is precisely why different outlets described the same report as both “close to expectations” and “hotter than expected.” Both readings are defensible, and the ambiguity is the whole reason Wednesday’s Fed decision is genuinely live.

Five losing weeks is the headline fact, not Friday’s small decline. The Nifty shed 499.60 points across the week to close at 23,398.10, and the Sensex gave up 1,733.67 points to 74,781.76. The selling was broad-based — weak global cues, foreign outflows, sectoral rotation out of risk, and rising volatility all pushing the same way. IT, metals, realty and autos were the biggest laggards. Financial services provided the stability, which is the one genuinely constructive structural detail in the week: the index’s heaviest sector is not the one breaking.

Flows. The most recent confirmable print has FIIs net sellers of roughly ₹931 crore in the cash segment against DIIs net buyers of roughly ₹1,968 crore. One source attributes these to Friday 11 September; yesterday’s edition reported the same shape of numbers as Thursday’s. The discrepancy is unresolved, so treat the figures as indicative of the pattern — persistent foreign selling absorbed by domestic institutions — rather than as a verified daily print. That pattern is what has kept the index from breaking down faster, and it is also why bounces keep failing: domestic buying defends, it does not chase.

Levels that matter on Tuesday. 23,200 is the line that turns this from a correction into something needing a new base. On the upside, 23,700–23,800 is what a genuine trend change has to reclaim. The realistic expectation for Tuesday is that the index opens to catch up with two sessions of global news and then goes quiet ahead of the Fed, which lands after that day’s close.

GIFT Nifty: no live read in this edition. The last sourced print before the break had September futures roughly 20 points higher, pointing to a mildly positive start — but that signal predates the weekend and the holiday and should be treated as expired, not as a pre-open indication for Tuesday.

Crude paused a hard rally rather than reversing it. Prices eased on Friday — one feed has Brent down 2.98% and WTI down 2.43% — after Iranian state media said Tehran would meet Gulf states in Oman to discuss the Strait of Hormuz. That is a diplomatic de-escalation headline, and the market treated it as one. But it is a headline about a scheduled conversation, not about an agreement, and the underlying supply risk is entirely intact.

This is the single most important variable for India in this briefing. Brent in the mid-hundreds is a direct import bill for a country that buys most of its oil abroad. It pressures the rupee, it feeds imported inflation, it squeezes margins for oil-marketing companies, aviation, paints and chemicals, and it constrains the RBI. It is also the reason the US 10-year has been pushing toward 5% and the reason a Fed hike is on the table at all. If Brent breaks below $100 and stays there, most of the bearish case in this briefing weakens at once. If the Oman talks collapse, most of it gets worse at once.

Gold is holding a one-month low, and the reason is rates rather than risk. With August CPI hardening the case for a Fed hike — one source puts implied odds near 90% — the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset rises, and paper positioning has been bracing for a hawkish outcome. Physical demand has been the steadier side of the market; futures traders have been the sellers. Silver was notably weaker, down about 2.6% on the session.

The tell to watch on Wednesday. Gold is currently trading the rate story, not the fear story. If the Fed hikes and gold rises anyway, that signals safe-haven demand has taken over from rate mathematics — which would be a risk-negative signal for equities, including Indian equities. If the Fed hikes and gold falls further, the market is still trading rates in an orderly way, and equities have less to fear from the metal’s message. Note that at roughly $4,380 gold sits well below its January record of $5,589.38, so this is a pullback within a larger move, not a breakdown.

Major Events to Watch (India & USA)

United States — this is the week that decides the quarter.

  • Wednesday 16 September — FOMC decision, Summary of Economic Projections, and the Chair’s press conference. A hike is priced by some feeds at roughly 90% probability, though other sources put it closer to 56%; that range could not be narrowed. Chair Kevin Warsh has been flagging inflation concerns publicly. The dot plot matters more than the decision — a hike that is framed as the last one is a very different market event from a hike that comes with more penciled in.
  • Wednesday 16 September — US Advance Retail Sales, landing the same day as the Fed. A strong print reinforces the hawkish case.
  • Bank of England and Bank of Japan decisions also fall in the same window, making this a genuinely global rates week rather than a US-only one. The BoJ in particular matters for carry-trade flows into emerging markets, India included.
  • The US 10-year yield near 5% is the level to watch throughout. A decisive break above it tightens global financial conditions and historically pressures emerging-market equity flows.

India.

  • Monday 14 September — markets closed for Ganesh Chaturthi. Equity, equity derivatives, currency derivatives, NDS-RST and tri-party repo segments are all suspended. Trading resumes Tuesday 15 September.
  • Tuesday 15 September — the reopen itself is the event. The index will price two sessions of global news in one open, and does so with the Fed still ahead rather than behind.
  • August CPI and IIP releases are expected in this general window, but the specific dates could not be confirmed within the research window. Given crude near $105, the imported-inflation component of the CPI print is the part worth reading closely.
  • Rupee and oil-import sensitivity remain the standing macro watch items for as long as Brent holds above $100.
