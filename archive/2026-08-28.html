Overall Call

Cautiously bullish — gap-up, but the keynote caps it

The setup is the mirror image of yesterday’s. On Thursday India sold off while waiting for confirmation — the Sensex fell 539 points to 76,933.59 and the Nifty slipped 0.48% to 24,090.85. Overnight, the confirmation arrived. Nvidia, which had initially dropped about 4% on its print, reversed hard and closed up 9.1% after guiding to roughly 70% revenue growth next fiscal year. The Nasdaq rose 1.6% to 26,541.35, the S&P 500 0.7% to 7,730.99, the Dow 0.2% to 53,569.44. India, in short, took the pain and missed the payoff — and now opens into it.

The breadth of the US move is what makes this more than a single-stock story. This was not Nvidia alone. Salesforce and CrowdStrike both lifted guidance, and a widely-followed software ETF climbed roughly 6.5% on the day. That matters for Indian IT specifically: broad enterprise-software strength is a cleaner read-through to Infosys, TCS and HCL than semiconductor capex ever is. Asian sentiment already reflected it — the Kospi rose about 1.5% and Shanghai roughly 1.1% in Thursday’s session, with the Bank of Korea delivering a second consecutive rate increase into the strength.

Two supports underneath, one risk overhead. The supports: crude remains soft, with Brent around $87 after an extended run of declines, which is the single most useful line on this page for an import-dependent economy; and domestic flows stayed firmly one-sided, with DIIs buying roughly ₹4,977 crore on Thursday against modest FII net selling of about ₹298 crore. The risk: Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote today. Consensus expects him not to telegraph the next rate decision, but that is an expectation, not a guarantee, and a hawkish surprise would land on a market already pricing meaningful odds of a hike by December.

What flips the call. To outright bullish: the gap-up holds through the first hour rather than fading as it did on Wednesday, IT leads on the software read-through, and the Nifty closes above 24,300 — that opens the 24,500–24,550 zone. To bearish: another failed opening pop, or a break of 24,150, which desks are naming as the level whose loss invites 24,000–23,950; a hawkish Warsh; or crude reversing higher if the Hormuz de-escalation talk unwinds. The honest base case sits between the two: a firm open that traders sell into ahead of a weekend that contains a Fed keynote.

Thursday was a give-back day, and the flow data explains why it was not worse. The Nifty’s 0.48% decline and the Sensex’s 539-point fall came on monthly F&O expiry, which reliably amplifies moves that would otherwise be routine. Underneath, the domestic bid held: DIIs bought roughly ₹4,977 crore in the cash segment against FII net selling of only about ₹298 crore. That is a nearly seventeen-to-one imbalance in favour of domestic money, and it has been the defining feature of this market for weeks.

FII positioning in derivatives is less friendly than the cash number suggests. Reported FII index futures activity ran around −1,871 while index options showed roughly +6,886 — a split that points to hedged rather than directionally bullish foreign positioning. Read the cash-segment calm alongside that, not instead of it.

Levels for the session. On the upside, a sustained move above 24,300 / 77,800 opens 24,500–24,550 on the Nifty and 78,300–78,500 on the Sensex. On the downside, 24,150 / 77,300 is the line desks are watching; a decisive loss of it points to 24,000–23,950 and 77,000–76,700. With Thursday’s close at 24,090.85, the index is already sitting below the first of those supports — which is precisely why the gap-up matters: it needs to reclaim 24,150 early and stay there.

Sectors to watch: IT on the Salesforce/CrowdStrike enterprise-software read-through, which is the cleanest overnight signal for Indian services exporters; oil marketing companies and paints on continued crude softness; and banks, which have neither a tailwind nor a headwind this morning and will likely follow the index rather than lead it.

Crude remains the quiet good news. Brent is around $87 and WTI around $81, both extending a multi-session drift lower. The driver reported through the week has been de-escalation optimism around the Strait of Hormuz, with talk of Iran and Oman converging on a framework, layered on a demand picture that has not tightened.

Why it matters more here than almost anywhere else. Every dollar off Brent flows through India’s import bill, the current-account arithmetic, the rupee, and inflation expectations — and it does so with far less lag than most macro variables. It is also the input that lets the RBI stay patient while the Fed argues with itself about hiking. The risk is symmetrical and fast-acting: if the Hormuz framework talk collapses, this support reverses within a session.

Brent and WTI levels are stated as approximate. Same-day intraday quotes could not be cross-checked against a second source within the research window; the most recent confirmed prints were from 26 August.

Gold is consolidating, not trending. Spot has been hovering in the $4,600–$4,650 area, with roughly $4,610 the most-cited figure into this morning. The technical frame is a $4,576–$4,698 range: a break above $4,700 opens the way toward $4,782, while a loss of $4,568 points to $4,500 and potentially $4,420.

The driver is entirely the rate path. With markets pricing something like a 38% chance of a September hike and above 70% by December, gold is caught between safe-haven demand and the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset in a tightening world. That is why it is ranging rather than breaking — and why Warsh’s keynote today is as much a gold event as an equity one. For context on the longer horizon, some sell-side research houses have year-end targets far above spot; those are forecasts, and the range above is what is actually trading.

The 2026 theme is financial innovation and its implications for payments and policy, and consensus expects him to stay off the near-term rate path — which is exactly what makes any deviation market-moving. The symposium runs 27–29 August.

  • India — the post-expiry reset. Thursday was monthly F&O expiry. The first session of a new series typically carries lighter positioning and cleaner directional moves, which slightly raises the odds that today’s gap-up either sticks or fails decisively rather than chopping.
  • Global — central bank divergence. The Bank of Korea’s second consecutive hike, against a Fed still debating direction, is widening the policy gap across Asia. Watch it through the currency channel rather than the equity one.
