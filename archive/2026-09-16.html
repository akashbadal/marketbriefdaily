Overall Call

Bearish — defensive into the Fed

Every input that matters to an oil-importing, yield-sensitive market is pointing the wrong way at once, and the one event that could resolve it lands after the Indian close.

The Fed decides tonight — 2:00 pm US Eastern, roughly 11:30 pm IST — and a 25 basis point hike is priced at around 90%. That is not a cut being taken away; it is a tightening cycle restarting, driven by an energy shock the Fed cannot control. Brent is back above $108 (+2.6%) and WTI is up close to 4% near $105 on the Saudi East–West pipeline outage and continued Middle East disruption. The US 10-year is sitting at 5.01%, the rupee has slipped to about 96.07, a seven-week low, and India’s own 10-year has climbed to roughly 7.10%.

Yesterday told the story cleanly. The Nifty opened up, was carried higher by a 4–5% move in IT, and then gave all of it back and more: Sensex −778 points to 74,004, Nifty −279.5 to 23,118.60 — the lowest close in five months. Financials fell 1.8%, autos 2%, realty 4%. When a market cannot hold a gap driven by its strongest sector, the selling is not sector-specific; it is macro. Bitcoin down 3.4% and ether down 4.7% overnight say the same thing about global risk appetite.

The honest position is that today is not a day to be adding risk before 11:30 pm IST. Rallies into the decision are more likely to be exit liquidity than a turn.

What flips the call. To bullish: the Fed holds, or hikes once and explicitly signals it is done — that would pull the 10-year back under 5% and let the rupee breathe; or credible news that the Saudi Petroline restarts early and Brent breaks back under $100; or the Nifty defends 23,000 with visibly positive breadth and DII buying stepping up again. To more bearish: the Fed hikes and leaves a second hike on the table, Brent presses $112–115, the US 10-year closes meaningfully above 5.05%, or the rupee breaks 96.50 — any of which likely opens the 22,700–23,000 zone on the Nifty.

Tuesday’s session in one line: the market opened higher on an IT rally and HDFC Bank, then erased all of it. HCL Tech (+4.4%), Infosys (+3.8%) and TCS (+2.8%) were the top performers and it made no difference — Nifty Financials fell 1.8%, autos 2%, and Nifty Realty dropped 4.04%, extending a 9.3% slide over seven sessions. Bharat Electronics fell 6% after analysts flagged downside risk to defence names.

Flows. The most recent confirmed cash-segment reading has FIIs net sellers of roughly ₹2,978 crore against DIIs net buyers of about ₹2,686 crore. That is the pattern of the last several weeks: domestic money absorbing foreign selling almost one-for-one, which cushions the index but does not turn it. With the rupee at 96 and US yields at 5%, the FII side of that equation is unlikely to improve before the Fed is out of the way.

Macro backdrop. August CPI came in at 4.82%, up from 4.44% in July — still inside the RBI’s band but moving the wrong way, and with crude at $108 the imported component gets worse before it gets better. The repo rate is 5.25%. A higher oil bill, a weaker rupee and a widening current account deficit are the same trade expressed three ways.

Levels worth watching. 23,000 on the Nifty is the round number and roughly where the five-month floor sits; below that, 22,700 is the next reference. On the upside, reclaiming 23,400 would be the first evidence that yesterday’s reversal was a flush rather than a continuation.

Both figures are approximate and feeds disagree by a dollar or more — readings seen this morning ranged from roughly $107.5 to $109.2 on Brent and $104.7 to $105.4 on WTI. Treat the level as “Brent in the high $108s, WTI just above $105” rather than as a precise print.

The driver is supply, not demand. Saudi Arabia’s East–West (Petroline) pipeline remains shut, removing the main route for exporting crude without transiting the Strait of Hormuz and forcing more volume through the strait exactly when shipping risk is elevated. Repairs are estimated at several weeks. This is now six months of Middle East disruption, and the market has started pricing the possibility that it persists into 2027 rather than resolving this quarter.

The second-order effects are already visible in the US: gasoline futures are up 4.6%, heating oil 5.9%, and pump prices are at their highest since early June. That is the transmission mechanism that has made a Fed hike the base case, and it is why oil and equities are the same trade right now.

Gold is the odd one out, and the reason is instructive. A geopolitical supply shock would normally be pure fuel for bullion, but the same shock has produced a 5% US 10-year and a near-certain rate hike, and non-yielding assets do badly against that. The dollar index at 99.66 (+0.27%) adds to it. Real-rate pressure is currently beating safe-haven demand.

Gold is roughly 23% below its record of about $5,600 set in late January 2026. The asymmetry into tonight is worth noting: if the Fed hikes and sounds hawkish, gold has further to fall; if it holds or signals it is finished, gold is one of the sharpest reversal candidates on the board.

Major Events to Watch (India & USA)

  • US FOMC decision — 2:00 pm ET / ~11:30 pm IST. The event of the week. Roughly 90% priced for a 25bp hike; J.P. Morgan expects the hike, Goldman Sachs has called it “very unlikely” and expects a hold through year-end. That split matters — it means a hold is a genuine surprise, not a non-event. The statement language on whether this is one-and-done will move markets more than the hike itself. Lands after the Indian close, so India trades the reaction on Thursday.
  • Middle East / Saudi Petroline. Any headline on repair timelines or further shipping disruption in the Strait of Hormuz is a same-day mover for Indian equities via the oil channel.
  • Rupee at 96. Watch for RBI presence. A defence of the level would be read as a floor; an unchecked break past 96.50 would pressure importers and add to the FII exit case.
  • Indian bond yields. The 10-year near 7.10%, a four-month high, with August CPI at 4.82% and oil at $108 — rate-cut expectations for the December policy are being unwound in real time.
  • US energy data. Crude inventories showed a surge in the latest API reading; the official weekly figure is the next check on whether the supply story is as tight as the price implies.

NSE Earnings Highlights

The calendar is genuinely quiet, and that is the honest finding rather than a gap. Mid-September falls between Indian reporting seasons: Q1 FY27 results concluded in August and Q2 FY27 reporting does not begin in earnest until the second week of October, when the IT majors traditionally open the season. No Nifty 50 constituent was confirmed as reporting today or this week within the research window for this edition.

What is moving individual large caps instead is news flow, not numbers:

  • HDFC Bank rose 1.3% yesterday on its leadership transition, having submitted two candidates to the RBI. The regulator’s response is the next catalyst.
  • Bharat Electronics fell 6% on analyst downgrades to the defence sector — worth watching whether that read-across extends to other defence PSUs.
  • IT majors (TCS, Infosys, HCL Tech, Tech Mahindra) have been the only sustained bid, helped by a weaker rupee mechanically improving margins. They are also the first to report next month, which makes the October guidance commentary the more important event.

On the forward view: brokerage estimates for FY27 Nifty earnings growth are unusually widely dispersed — JM Financial around 17%, BofA Securities around 8.5%. That spread is itself a signal about how much macro uncertainty is in the system.
