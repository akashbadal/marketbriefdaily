Overall Call

Sideways — capped by event risk

The market got the bullish catalyst it wanted and could not hold it. Nvidia reported after the US close on Wednesday and the numbers were not merely good, they were a blowout: revenue of $96.2 billion against roughly $92.2 billion expected, up 106% year on year, with current-quarter guidance of $108 billion versus about $104.2 billion consensus. Asia opened on that optimism. The Nifty opened up 82 points at 24,289 and the Sensex was up over 100 points in pre-open. By 10:00 IST both were back below flat — the Sensex around 77,380 (−0.12%) and the Nifty around 24,188 (−0.08%), roughly 300 points off the day’s Sensex high.

A rally that fails on the best possible news is information. What is capping it is the US rate picture. Wall Street closed lower on Wednesday — Dow −0.21%, S&P 500 −0.02% at 7,675.70, Nasdaq −0.08% at 26,130 — because a US inflation gauge printed above the Fed’s target and revived talk of a rate hike this calendar year. Markets are pricing roughly one-in-three odds of a September hike, and the Jackson Hole symposium opens today, with Chair Kevin Warsh delivering his first keynote as Fed Chair tomorrow. Nobody wants a large position into that.

The genuinely constructive leg is crude. Brent has now fallen for a fifth consecutive session to around $87, on optimism that Iran and Oman are converging on a framework for the Strait of Hormuz. For an import-dependent economy that is the single most useful thing on this page, and it is why the call is sideways rather than bearish. Domestic flows are also still supportive: DIIs bought roughly ₹6,425 crore on Wednesday against FII net buying of about ₹503 crore.

What flips the call. To bullish: a close back above 24,300 with the Nvidia read-through actually sticking in IT and the broader tech complex, ideally with Brent breaking under $85 and Warsh striking a neutral-to-dovish tone tomorrow — that combination would open the 24,490 area. To bearish: a break of 24,050, which is the level domestic desks are naming as the line that keeps the bullish structure intact; or a hawkish Warsh that pushes September hike odds meaningfully above 50%; or crude reversing higher if the Hormuz framework talk collapses.

All three major indices closed lower on Wednesday, ahead of Nvidia’s post-close report, as investors weighed a US inflation gauge that remained above the Federal Reserve’s target — keeping alive the possibility of a rate hike before year-end. The market was also digesting a Meta legal ruling during the session. Nvidia shares then jumped in after-hours trade on the results.

A note on the GIFT Nifty read: it was quoted at 24,346 and described as down 84 points, while separate pre-open commentary called for a roughly 35-point positive open. Those two framings are hard to reconcile and the actual open was positive. Treat the GIFT figure as approximate.

Beneath the index, the tape is better than the headline. Broader markets are outperforming — the Nifty MidCap and Nifty SmallCap were both up, around 0.36% and 0.31% respectively. IT and auto led the early move, consistent with the Nvidia read-through. On the Sensex, Bajaj Finance, Sun Pharma and Tech Mahindra were among the gainers; Power Grid, HDFC Bank and NTPC were among the decliners. Pharma has been the standout sector this week, with Divi’s Laboratories and Laurus Labs hitting new highs, and cement names have also been firm.

Flows. On Wednesday, FIIs were net buyers of roughly ₹503 crore in the cash segment and DIIs net buyers of roughly ₹6,425 crore. The asymmetry is the familiar one and it is the market’s main structural support: domestic money is absorbing supply, foreign money is present but not committed. Today’s figures are not yet available.

Levels. Support at 24,050 is the line domestic technical desks are naming as the one that keeps the bullish structure intact. Above, 24,300 is the immediate hurdle, with roughly 24,490 cited as the upside objective if it clears. The rupee opened at 95.43 to the dollar, two paise weaker than Wednesday’s 95.41 — essentially flat.

Crude Oil

Crude is the quiet good news. Brent has fallen for a fifth consecutive session, trading around $86.40–$86.95 depending on the contract and feed — ICE September futures were quoted at $86.40, while a separate spot reference showed roughly $86.93, down about 1.0%. Call it approximately $87 rather than treating any single figure as precise. WTI was last around $80.8, itself down nearly 2% on the prior session.

The driver is diplomatic rather than fundamental. Traders are pricing optimism that Iran and Oman are converging on a framework to keep the Strait of Hormuz functional, and a round of fresh US economic sanctions on Iran landed less aggressively than markets had feared. That has been steadily draining the geopolitical risk premium that had crude near one-month highs a week ago. For India this is the most directly favourable input on the page — it eases the import bill, the current-account arithmetic and the inflation path simultaneously. The risk is symmetrical: this is a headline-driven decline, and headlines reverse.

Gold

Gold is consolidating near recent highs. Spot is above $4,600, having reached a more-than-three-month high of about $4,696 earlier in the week before easing back toward $4,620. Feeds place it in a roughly $4,577–$4,698 band, so the level is best described as approximate. Domestically, MCX gold rose about ₹320 in the prior session and gold futures were trading around 0.88% higher this morning; silver futures were notably stronger, up around 2.2%.

The driver is entirely the Fed path. Markets have been scaling back bets on a September rate hike — odds fell to roughly 35% from 40% after a softer US producer price reading — and CME pricing puts the probability of rates being held at 3.50–3.75% in September at about 61%. Lower hike odds reduce the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset. That makes gold an unusually direct read on tomorrow’s Warsh keynote: a hawkish tone is the clearest single threat to the metal from here.

Major Events to Watch — India & USA

  • Jackson Hole opens today (27–29 August). Theme: “Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy.” Roughly 120 officials and economists from more than 70 countries attend. This is the dominant event on the calendar and positioning ahead of it explains a good deal of today’s reluctance to hold gains.
  • Kevin Warsh’s first keynote as Fed Chair — Friday 28 August, 10:00 ET. Warsh took office in May. With September hike odds around one-in-three, the market has no settled read on his reaction function, and twenty minutes at the podium could be the tiebreaker. This is the single highest-variance scheduled event of the week for Indian markets via the dollar, yields and flows.
  • US inflation above target. Wednesday’s gauge came in above the Fed’s target and is what pushed Wall Street red into the Nvidia print. A hike-risk narrative alongside a booming AI capex cycle is an awkward combination for risk assets, and it is the core tension in the tape right now.
  • Iran / Strait of Hormuz. The Iran–Oman framework talk is what is driving crude lower. Any breakdown reverses the single most India-friendly input on this page.
  • India — monsoon deficit. Cumulative rainfall is running about 13% below normal with 14 states recording deficits. Not a market-moving headline today, but a slow-burn input into rural demand, kharif output and the food-inflation path that is worth carrying forward.
  • US immigration policy. The US has paused immigrant visa applications worldwide. Watch for read-through to Indian IT services staffing and commentary, particularly given IT led this morning’s early gains.
  • India M&A. Crisil reports India Inc. deal volumes have roughly doubled, with M&A emerging as a core growth strategy — structural context rather than a same-day catalyst.

NSE Earnings Highlights

The Q1 FY27 season is effectively over. More than 760 companies had reported by end-July and the reporting window has now largely closed. No Nifty 50 constituent was confirmed as reporting today within the research window, so the honest answer is that there is no index-level earnings catalyst on the Indian calendar this session.

The earnings event that actually matters today is American. Nvidia’s Q2 (quarter ended 26 July) is a clean beat on every line: revenue $96.2 billion versus roughly $92.2 billion expected, up 18% sequentially and 106% year on year; data-centre revenue $89 billion against about $85.7 billion expected, up 18% quarter on quarter with strength in both hyperscale and ACIE; non-GAAP EPS of $2.22 against expectations of $2.06–$2.09; and current-quarter guidance of $108 billion ±2% versus about $104.2 billion consensus. Management characterised demand as accelerating. For Indian markets the transmission is indirect — IT services sentiment, the broader AI capex narrative, and global risk appetite — and this morning’s fade suggests that transmission is weaker than the headline numbers would imply.

Stocks in focus on domestic news flow today: ICICI Prudential AMC, Tata Power, Bharat Electronics, Adani Energy Solutions and GR Infraprojects. Elsewhere, Hindustan Zinc rose around 5% after Jefferies flagged an earnings boost from favourable zinc and silver prices; Vedanta, FSN E-Commerce (Nykaa) and Navin Fluorine are on analyst watchlists; and Reliance Jio received tax relief as a tribunal deleted a ₹11,003 crore disallowance.

This section is company-news-led rather than results-led because the results calendar is genuinely empty at the index level, not because the calendar could not be checked.
