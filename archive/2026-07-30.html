Overall Call

Bearish

Everything that matters happened after India went home. The Nifty closed Wednesday at 24,250.20, up 264.85 points or 1.10%, and the Sensex at 77,654.60, up 888.68 or 1.16% — a strong, broad session. Then three things landed in sequence: fighting resumed in the Middle East, crude spiked almost 7% in a day, and the Fed held rates while three officials dissented in favour of a hike. Wall Street fell hard. India is walking into this morning carrying yesterday's optimism and none of yesterday's information.

The oil move is the part that hurts India specifically. Brent jumped roughly 6.9% to about $89 and traded back above $90 intraday; WTI rose about 6.2% to roughly $84. This is a complete round trip — only two days ago this briefing led with oil's worst three-day run in six years and $84 Brent as a gift to a net importer. That gift has been withdrawn. Joint US–Saudi airstrikes on Iran-backed militias in Iraq, an Iranian ballistic-missile response against US forces, continued tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz and Houthi strikes on Saudi pipeline infrastructure have rebuilt the risk premium in 48 hours. For India the transmission is immediate: import bill, rupee, headline inflation, OMC marketing margins, paints, tyres, aviation and logistics all move the wrong way.

The Fed made it worse, not better. The FOMC voted 9–3 to hold at 3.50–3.75%, but Hammack, Kashkari and Logan all dissented for a 25 bp increase — the first triple dissent since September 2016, and notably it came from the hawkish side. A hold that arrives with three votes for tightening is not a hold the market can lean on. US yields rose, and the Dow fell 1,153.18 points, or 2.19%, to 51,594.14. That combination — higher US yields plus higher crude — is the least friendly macro pairing there is for Indian equities.

Why not violently bearish. Two offsets, and they are real. First, foreign investors were net buyers of ₹2,981.90 cr in the cash segment on Wednesday against DII buying of ₹998.00 cr — FIIs on the bid is a change from the pattern that has capped this market for weeks, though that print predates the Fed. Second, the pre-open signal is milder than the cues deserve: GIFT Nifty is reading flat-to-marginally-weak, around −0.11%, rather than gapping down 1%+. Domestic earnings have also held up, and India's own inflation and rate picture is more comfortable than America's. See the Indian Markets section — our GIFT reads conflict today and we are not treating any of them as decisive.

Net: expect a lower open, pressure concentrated in oil-sensitive consumption, aviation and OMC names, relative strength in upstream (ONGC, Oil India) and possibly IT on a weaker rupee, and a session that trades the Hormuz headlines more than the earnings. Wednesday's 24,250 close is now the level to defend; 24,000 is the line below it that matters.

What flips the call: turns bullish if Brent falls back under $85 on any de-escalation headline, if Nifty holds 24,150 through the first hour and FIIs stay on the bid, or if Bajaj Finance and Tata Steel deliver clean beats that give the index something domestic to hold. Turns materially worse if Brent clears $95 or Hormuz transit is actually disrupted rather than threatened, if Nifty loses 24,000, if the rupee breaks to a new low, or if today's US session extends Wednesday's fall and the hike now priced for September starts getting pulled forward.

A complete round trip in 48 hours. Brent rose about 6.9% to roughly $89.43 and headlines had it back above $90 intraday; WTI gained about 6.2% to around $84.18 from an $79.11 open. Two days ago this was a market pricing peace at $84 and falling. It is now pricing war again.

What changed. Joint US–Saudi airstrikes hit Iran-backed militias in Iraq — reportedly killing at least 20 fighters, and marking the first time Saudi Arabia has publicly acknowledged a combat role in this conflict. Iran responded with a ballistic-missile barrage against US forces in the region. Separately, Iranian forces have repeatedly attacked tankers transiting the Strait of Hormuz this month, and Houthi forces say they targeted Saudi pipeline infrastructure feeding the Red Sea export terminal at Yanbu. The June memorandum of understanding that was supposed to end the war within 60 days has effectively collapsed.

Note on the level. Feeds disagree by a dollar or more depending on contract and timestamp, and one headline had Brent above $90 while another quoted $89.43. Treat $89–90 as approximate. The direction and magnitude — a roughly 7% single-day gain — are consistent across sources.

For India: unambiguously negative, and the most important number on this page. Every $10 on Brent is a meaningful hit to the current account and roughly a measurable addition to headline CPI with a lag. Watch OMCs (marketing margins compress), paints and tyres (crude derivatives), aviation (ATF), cement and logistics (freight). Upstream producers ONGC and Oil India are the offset. The tail risk is not the price — it is actual physical disruption at Hormuz, which is a different order of event entirely.

Safe-haven demand is doing the work, and it is fighting the Fed. Two forces are pulling in opposite directions. Renewed Middle East fighting is a straightforward bid for gold, and it is winning. Against it, a Fed that just held with three votes for a hike means higher-for-longer real rates and a firmer dollar, both traditionally gold-negative. Major Events to Watch

USA — the FOMC aftermath. The decision is done: 9–3 to hold at 3.50–3.75%, with Beth Hammack, Neel Kashkari and Lorie Logan dissenting in favour of a 25 bp hike. Three dissents is the most since September 2016 and Kashkari's hawkish turn was the surprise. The press conference offered no commitment on September, citing easing financial conditions, low unemployment and above-target inflation as reasons to stay put, while flagging labour-market downside as the reason to stay nimble. One source describes this as the second meeting under new Chair Kevin Warsh while another attributes press-conference remarks to Chair Powell — we could not resolve that discrepancy in the window and have therefore described the substance without attributing it. Verify before quoting. The market's read now shifts to September, and the Middle East escalation makes the inflation side of the Fed's problem harder.

Geopolitics — the Iran conflict, and it is the dominant variable. Direct Iranian missile fire on US forces, Saudi Arabia entering openly as a combatant, and repeated attacks on Hormuz shipping put this at a different level than a week ago. Every Indian asset — equities, rupee, bonds, gold — is now a function of these headlines. Watch for any confirmed interruption to Hormuz transit; that is the scenario that reprices everything.

India. No major scheduled domestic macro release was identified within today's window — the domestic drivers are earnings and the crude pass-through. Flagged as a gap: the Indian economic calendar was not exhaustively checked. Note also that monthly F&O expiry sits at the end of this week, which can add volatility independent of news.

Season so far. Roughly 509 companies have reported Q1 FY27. Revenue growth is healthy, but the gap between sales growth and profit growth says costs are still eating margins — a gap that today's oil move will widen next quarter. Domestic investment, manufacturing, infrastructure and financial services are outperforming; export-oriented and consumer-facing names are struggling. Standouts include IDFC First Bank with a record quarterly PAT of ₹1,075 cr, up 132.4% year on year, and AU Small Finance Bank with net profit up 37% to ₹796 cr.

The uncomfortable arithmetic today: India's market rallied 1.1% on domestic earnings resilience, and then the two variables it cannot control — the price of imported crude and the cost of American money — both moved against it within hours. Earnings quality does not protect against that in the short run.
