Overall Call

Bearish

Two forces are pulling the same direction, and that is what makes today different from a normal soft open. A deepening global bond sell-off and a fresh crude spike are hitting simultaneously. For an oil-importing, capital-importing emerging market, that is close to the worst available combination — it raises the import bill, widens the current account, lifts the domestic cost of capital, and makes the alternative (US Treasuries) more attractive all at once.

The bond move is the headline. The US 10-year Treasury yield pushed to 4.81% in Asian trade, its highest since November 2023. India’s benchmark 10-year briefly topped 7% for the first time in three months, with the 6.94% 2036 bond up about 3bps at 6.9883%. When the risk-free rate repriced this fast, equity multiples follow.

Crude is the accelerant. WTI is around $90.7 and Brent above $90, the highest since late July, with the Strait of Hormuz still effectively closed to routine commercial traffic. India sources roughly 60% of its crude from the Middle East and more than half its LNG is Gulf-linked, much of it Brent-indexed. This is not an abstract geopolitical headline for this market; it is a direct input cost.

The Fed has flipped from cut to hike. CME FedWatch now prices a 68% chance of a 25bp hike this month, up from 41% a week ago. The FOMC meets 15–16 September. A market pricing a hike into an oil shock will not pay up for emerging-market beta.

The overnight and morning tape confirms it. Wall Street closed broadly lower on Tuesday with the Nasdaq worst (−1.03%) — note the shape has changed from Monday, when the Dow led losses. This is now a duration event, not just an energy one. Asia followed hard, with the Nikkei down 2.81%. GIFT Nifty signalled 23,939.5, −0.47% against a 24,052 reference, and the Sensex and Nifty duly opened sharply lower.

One thing does not fit, and it is worth watching. India VIX is around 11.49 and essentially unchanged. That is a remarkably relaxed volatility reading for a session with a bond rout, $90 crude and a live Fed hike risk. Either the market is correctly treating this as an orderly repricing rather than a panic, or protection is underpriced going into the FOMC. That gap is the main risk to sizing today.

What flips the call. To bullish: Hormuz de-escalation with Brent back under $85, the US 10-year retreating below 4.65%, September hike odds falling back under 50%, India’s 10-year holding below 7%, and the Nifty reclaiming and holding 24,050–24,100. To more deeply bearish: Brent decisively through $95, the US 10-year clearing 4.90%, hike odds above 80%, a sustained break of 23,800 on the Nifty, sharp rupee weakness on the oil bill, or India VIX spiking through 14 — which would signal the complacency above is unwinding. Sideways: crude stabilising near $90 without escalating and yields going quiet ahead of the FOMC, leaving the index to grind in a 23,850–24,050 range on thin conviction.

The open was sharply lower, as signalled. GIFT Nifty pointed to a weak start at 23,939.5 (−0.47%) against a reference of 24,052, and the Sensex and Nifty both opened materially down on the global cues.

The bond market is the real story here, not the equity gap. Indian government bonds sank early, with the benchmark 10-year briefly clearing 7% for the first time in three months. The mechanism is direct: a global debt rout plus an oil spike raises both the imported inflation risk and the fiscal cost of borrowing. Rising domestic yields compress equity valuations and hurt rate-sensitive sectors — banks on treasury mark-to-market, NBFCs on funding cost, real estate and autos on demand.

The foreign-flow channel is the one to watch over the next few sessions. When US yields rise this fast, capital tends to migrate back into dollar-denominated assets, and emerging markets like India historically see foreign portfolio flows reverse. Crude Oil

Crude is above $90 and at its highest since late July. WTI is quoted around $90.72 (+0.55%) and Brent above $90, after a move of more than 5% on Tuesday. Treat these as approximate — the quoted Brent and WTI levels imply an unusually narrow spread between the two grades, which suggests at least one feed is stale or mismatched on timing.

The driver is Hormuz, and it is a supply-availability story rather than a sentiment story. The Strait has been effectively closed to routine commercial shipping since late July, with traffic running near 10 vessels a day against a pre-crisis baseline of roughly 88–130, and convoys moving under naval escort. Iranian forces resumed attacks on shipping in July after the June memorandum of understanding broke down.

For India this is a first-order macro variable. Roughly 60% of India’s crude imports come from the Middle East, more than half of its LNG is Gulf-linked, and a meaningful share of that LNG is Brent-indexed — so a crude spike lifts gas contract prices at the same time. Add surging shipping insurance and freight costs and the effect compounds through the current account and imported inflation. This is the mechanism by which a Gulf headline becomes an Indian equity problem.

Gold

Gold is falling, which is the counterintuitive part of today. Spot is quoted at $4,374.54, down about 1.60% on one feed and $4,302.36 on another — a spread of over $70 for the same session. The direction is reliable; the level is not. On Tuesday, physical gold slid to a two-week low, down from a three-month high set only last week.

Why gold is not acting as a safe haven. On a day of geopolitical escalation you would ordinarily expect gold to rally. It is falling because the rate channel is overwhelming the safe-haven channel: surging Treasury yields raise the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset, and rising odds of a September Fed hike strengthen the dollar. When real yields move this sharply, they dominate gold’s geopolitical bid.

This is diagnostically useful. Gold falling alongside equities and bonds tells you today is being traded as a rates and inflation event, not as a flight to safety. If gold turns and rallies while equities keep falling, the market has switched to genuine risk aversion — that would be a meaningful regime change to watch for.

Major Events to Watch (India & USA)

  • FOMC, 15–16 September. The dominant scheduled event. Markets price a 68% chance of a 25bp hike, up from 41% a week ago. Every US inflation and labour print between now and then will move this number, and Indian equities with it.
  • The global bond rout itself. The US 10-year at 4.81% and India’s at ~7% are the live variables. A further leg higher pressures equities directly; stabilisation would relieve much of today’s selling.
  • US–Iran hostilities and the Strait of Hormuz. The Strait remains effectively closed. Any escalation, or conversely a credible de-escalation or renewed mediation, is the largest single swing factor for crude and therefore for the Indian macro picture.
  • Rupee and the oil import bill. With crude above $90 and foreign flows at risk of reversing, INR is under pressure from both sides. Watch for RBI intervention signals.
  • Indian inflation and RBI positioning. A sustained crude spike feeds into imported inflation with a lag, which constrains the RBI’s room to ease and reinforces the domestic yield move.

NSE Earnings Highlights

The Q1 FY27 reporting season is essentially complete, so there is little fresh company news to trade today — which is precisely why the macro is driving everything.

The aggregate Nifty 50 picture is contested, and the disagreement is itself informative. One read of Q1 FY27 describes healthy revenue and operating-profit growth but softer net-profit growth as rising costs squeeze margins. Another reports aggregate PAT up 22% on an earnings beat. These are hard to reconcile and likely reflect different sample sets and treatment of one-offs. The defensible summary: topline growth is solid, margins are under pressure from input costs, and the bottom-line number depends heavily on what you include. Given crude at $90, the margin-squeeze framing is the more relevant one going forward. Sector strength was reported in industrials, automobiles, energy and financial services.

NSE (the exchange, unlisted) reported a clean quarter: revenue from operations ₹4,560.41 crore (+13.09% YoY), EBITDA ₹4,332.03 crore (+10.33%), and PAT ₹3,120.08 crore (+6.71%) versus ₹2,923.85 crore. The SEBI settlement payment was approved at the 30 July board meeting and does not drag on this quarter’s profit. Note the pattern — revenue growing faster than EBITDA, which grows faster than PAT — the same margin compression visible across the index.
