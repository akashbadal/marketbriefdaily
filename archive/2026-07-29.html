Overall Call

Sideways with an upside tilt

The dominant fact this morning is the collapse in crude, and India is the single biggest beneficiary in the world. Brent fell 4.8% on Tuesday to close near $84 a barrel, capping oil's worst three-day stretch in more than six years. The driver is diplomacy: Washington has paused strikes and is talking to Tehran, and traders are unwinding the Strait of Hormuz risk premium that had crude near $100 barely a week ago. For a large net importer, a $16 move down in a week is a direct transfer to the current account, to inflation, and to margins at OMCs, paints, aviation, tyres and logistics.

The domestic tape is quiet, not weak. Nifty closed Tuesday at 23,985.35, down 10.60 points, and the Sensex at 76,765.92, down 69.86 — flat in every meaningful sense, with IT outperforming on a day when North Asian technology was being sold hard. Japan's Nikkei fell 3.95% and India did not follow it. That refusal to import Asian tech stress is now two sessions old and is the most useful signal on the board.

Why not outright bullish. Three restraints. First, the Fed decides tonight at 2:00 pm ET — roughly 11:30 pm IST, after the Indian close — so today's session is traded blind into an event, which usually caps conviction and volume. A hold at 3.50–3.75% is the base case at about 62% on CME FedWatch, but nearly 38% is priced for a hike, which is an unusually live tail. Second, FII flows are still negative: the most recent confirmed print showed foreign investors selling ₹1,688 cr in cash against DII buying of ₹2,329 cr. Third, our pre-open signal is genuinely conflicted today (see Indian Markets) — one feed reads GIFT Nifty as marginally negative, another quotes a level well above Tuesday's Nifty close. We are not treating either as decisive.

Net: expect a flat-to-firm open, leadership from oil-linked and consumption names rather than from the index heavyweights, and a drifting, low-conviction afternoon as desks square up before the Fed. Respect 24,000 as the pivot — the Nifty has now spent several sessions failing to hold it.

What flips the call: turns bullish if Nifty takes 24,000 and closes above it, Brent stays under $85, and Asian Paints and Dabur deliver clean margins despite high input costs. Turns bearish if the Iran talks stall and crude gaps back above $92, if Nifty loses 23,800, if the Nikkei's tech unwind broadens beyond semiconductors into the wider Asian tape today, or if positioning ahead of the Fed tilts toward the hike scenario during the session.

US (settled). Tuesday was a rotation, not a rally. The Dow surged 537.24 points or 1.03% to 52,747.32 on cheap oil and strong earnings, the S&P 500 added 0.21% to 7,428.78, and the Nasdaq slipped 0.22% to 24,876.91 as money came out of semiconductors and went almost everywhere else. That is the cleanest expression of the current regime: falling crude is repricing the industrial, transport and consumer half of the market upward while the AI-capex question keeps a lid on the technology half.

Asia (Tuesday's closes — today's live levels not confirmed). Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 3.95% to 62,364.92, extending the memory and AI-chip unwind that hit Korea a day earlier. Hong Kong went the other way, with the Hang Seng up 0.41% to 25,310.85, and Shanghai fell 1.16% to 3,813.32. Australia's ASX 200 rose 0.60% to 8,947.80. The split matters: this is a semiconductor de-rating, not a broad risk-off, and markets without heavy chip weight are shrugging it off. We could not verify this morning's live Asian prints inside today's window — treat the direction as indicative and the levels as yesterday's.

Europe (look-ahead). Not yet open. Europe has modest semiconductor weight outside ASML and is a large net energy importer, so the crude collapse should read as a clear positive for the FTSE 100 and DAX. One feed noted firm European trade as a support for Asian sentiment into today.

This is the story of the week. Brent futures fell 4.8% on Tuesday to close around $84.09, and WTI dropped about 4% to roughly $79.28 — the worst three-day run for oil in more than six years. A week ago Brent was near $100.

The driver is entirely geopolitical de-escalation. The US has paused military strikes on Iran to give negotiations room, and the President has described the talks as good with a good chance of resolution. Traders are unwinding the Strait of Hormuz supply-disruption premium as fast as they built it. Both sides continuing to hold off on attacks is what is holding the move.

Note on the level. Feeds disagree materially this morning — Brent closing quotes ranged from $82.82 to $84.09 depending on the timestamp and contract. Treat $84 as approximate. The direction, however, is not in dispute.

For India: unambiguously positive. Lower crude eases the import bill, cools headline and core inflation, supports the rupee, and lifts margins for oil marketing companies, paints, tyres, aviation and paint-adjacent chemicals. It is also a mild negative for upstream names like ONGC and Oil India. The risk is symmetry — a premium that fell this fast on talks can return just as fast if the talks break.

Gold fell about 1% on Tuesday to around $4,030 an ounce, holding below $4,100. Quotes across providers clustered between $4,026 and $4,037 depending on timestamp — the level is approximate.

Two forces, same direction. A firmer US dollar and caution ahead of tonight's Fed decision both weighed. The safe-haven bid is also draining as the Middle East de-escalates — the same trade that is pulling crude down is pulling gold down, and that correlation is the cleanest evidence that this is a geopolitical-risk unwind rather than a growth story. A hawkish Fed tonight would extend the fall; a dovish read or any breakdown in the Iran talks would reverse it quickly.

Major Events to Watch

USA — the FOMC decision, 2:00 pm ET (about 11:30 pm IST). This lands after the Indian close, so it sets up Thursday rather than today. Consensus is a hold at 3.50–3.75%, which would be the fifth consecutive pause; CME FedWatch put that at roughly 62% as of last week, with about 38% pricing a hike to 3.75–4.00%. That is a live tail by recent standards. The bigger signal is the trajectory — after June, nine of eighteen officials projected at least one hike this year and only one a cut, and roughly 40% of the market expects two quarter-point hikes by December. The statement language, not the decision, is what will move Thursday's open in Mumbai.

Geopolitics — US–Iran talks. The single largest swing factor for Indian assets right now. Progress keeps crude falling and the rupee supported; a breakdown reverses both violently. Worth watching headlines through the session.

India. No major scheduled domestic macro release was identified within today's research window — the domestic driver today is earnings, not data. Flagged as a gap: this run was time-compressed and the Indian economic calendar was not exhaustively checked.

NSE Earnings Highlights

Today, 29 July — a consumer-goods day. One of the busier results days of the season, and unusually concentrated in FMCG.

  • Asian Paints — brokerages look for revenue growth of roughly 14–17% year on year, helped by price hikes and dealer stocking ahead of them. Margins are expected flat to slightly lower because crude-linked raw material costs rose during the quarter. Note the irony: the crude collapse is a next-quarter story for this stock, not a this-print story. The guidance commentary matters more than the number.
  • Dabur — the company has pointed to roughly double-digit revenue and profit growth, India volumes up about 5%, and rural demand outpacing urban for a second straight quarter. Watch whether the operating margin held near its historical 20–21% against high input costs.
  • Colgate-Palmolive India and P&G Hygiene also report, completing the consumer block.

Tomorrow, 30 July — considerably bigger. Bajaj Finance, Tata Steel, Mahindra & Mahindra, Sun Pharma, Maruti Suzuki and Swiggy all report. That is the day that will actually move the index, and several of those names (Maruti, M&M, Tata Steel) are direct beneficiaries of cheaper energy and inputs.

Rural demand outpacing urban for a second consecutive quarter is worth more attention than it usually gets — combined with falling fuel costs, it is the more durable of the two consumption signals on the board today.
