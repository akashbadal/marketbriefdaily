Overall Call

Sideways — upward bias, low conviction

The headline looks strong and the internals do not. The Nifty opened above 23,500 and the Sensex added roughly 400 points, but essentially all of that came from one sector: Nifty IT jumped about 5%, with HCL Tech, Tech Mahindra and Infosys each up more than 5%. The trigger is offshore and slightly unusual — Anthropic’s CEO publicly called for a slowdown in frontier AI development and other technology figures backed him, which knocked US AI names lower overnight but simultaneously relieved the disruption fear that has hung over Indian IT services all year. What hurts Nasdaq helps Infosys, at least for a session.

Underneath that, the market is not participating. Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100 are both slightly negative, Nifty Realty is the worst sector, and the pre-open reading (Nifty 23,576, +0.76%) was stronger than the actual open — sellers met the gap. Meanwhile the macro floor is genuinely hostile: Brent is near $108 after the Saudi East–West pipeline shutdown, the US 10-year briefly touched 5% for the first time since 2023, the rupee opened 18 paise weaker at 95.74, and August WPI came in at 9.92%. For an oil-importing, current-account-sensitive market, that is three headwinds at once.

Add that the Fed decides tomorrow with a hike live, and the honest read is that today is a narrow relief rally inside a five-week downtrend, not a turn. Treat strength as tactical.

What flips the call. To bullish: the IT bid broadens into banks and autos, breadth turns positive into the afternoon, Brent slips back under $100 on credible news that Petroline restarts sooner than the five-to-six-week estimate, and the Fed holds tomorrow with dovish guidance. To bearish: the IT pop fades and the index gives back the gap, Brent presses $110+, the US 10-year closes decisively above 5%, the rupee breaks past 96, or the Fed hikes and signals more — any one of which would likely take the Nifty back to test the 23,000–23,200 support band technicians are watching.

Crude Oil

This is the single most important input to the Indian tape right now, and it is moving fast.

Saudi Arabia shut its East–West crude pipeline — Petroline, roughly 750 miles, about 7 million bpd of full pumping capacity — as a precaution after repeated drone attacks launched from Iraq. The line is what has let Saudi exports reach the Red Sea while the Strait of Hormuz is disrupted by the US–Iran conflict, so its loss is strategic rather than incremental: reporting suggests up to 4% of global supply is at risk if it is not restarted within days. Repair estimates range from partial resumption soon to five or six weeks.

Context that makes it worse: Saudi Arabia told OPEC its August output had already fallen to about 6.2 million bpd, against 10.9 million bpd in February before the war. Brent is up roughly 19% in a month and around 60% year on year. For India this is the textbook negative — imported inflation, a wider current account deficit, pressure on the rupee, and margin compression for oil marketing companies, paints, tyres and aviation.

Gold

Gold is soft, which is counterintuitive against a live geopolitical supply shock and tells you what is actually driving the metal right now: rates and the dollar, not fear.

Spot sits around $4,284–4,285 an ounce after easing on Monday, down from roughly $4,375 at the start of the month and near a one-month low. The oil spike has been read as inflation fuel, which has hardened expectations of a Fed hike; the dollar firmed to an over one-week high, making bullion more expensive outside the US. Silver has been hit harder, dropping toward $63 with a 2.7% fall on one session.

The setup is unstable in both directions: if the Fed hikes and sounds hawkish, gold likely stays pressured; if it holds, the safe-haven bid from the Gulf situation has room to reassert quickly.

Major Events to Watch (India & USA)

United States

  • FOMC meeting begins today, decision Wednesday 16 September. This is the event of the week. The market is weighing a hold at 3.50–3.75% against a 25bp hike — which would be the first increase since 2023. Expectations are genuinely split: CME FedWatch-based readings have been quoted anywhere from about 66% to 87% probability of a hike depending on the day and the source, while at least one major house has publicly argued for a 90% chance of no move. Treat the probability as contested, not settled.
  • August retail sales, released this morning US time, ahead of the decision — the last significant consumer read the committee sees.
  • US 10-year Treasury yield around 5%. A decisive close above is a global risk event in its own right, independent of the Fed.
  • US–Iran conflict and the Gulf supply picture. Any further attack on Saudi infrastructure, or credible news on Petroline repairs, moves oil and therefore moves India.

India

  • August WPI inflation came in at 9.92% (released Monday), up from 9.78% in July. Fuel and power inflation surged to 22.93% from 20.05%; manufactured goods 8.37%; WPI food 7.05%. Primary articles eased to 7.76%, the one soft spot. Near-10% wholesale inflation with Brent at $108 is not a combination that gives the RBI room.
  • Rupee at 95.74 and weakening for a fifth session. A break of 96 would be a fresh psychological marker and would likely accelerate FII outflows.
  • NSE IPO bidding opens 17 September at a ₹1,700–1,785 band — a meaningful liquidity event for the secondary market later this week.
  • Technical level to watch: analysts are flagging 23,000–23,200 as crucial Nifty support, with the possibility of a near-term rebound from here.

NSE Earnings Highlights

There is no meaningful Indian earnings flow today. Mid-September falls between reporting quarters for NSE-listed companies — Q1 FY27 results concluded in August, and the Q2 FY27 season does not begin in earnest until the second week of October, when the IT majors traditionally open the calendar. No Nifty 50 constituent is scheduled to report today.

That matters for how you read today’s tape: the 5% move in Nifty IT is entirely sentiment and re-rating, not a response to numbers. There is no earnings evidence behind it, which is a reason to hold it loosely. The first real test of the AI-disruption thesis for Indian IT comes with the October results and, more importantly, with management commentary on deal pipelines.
