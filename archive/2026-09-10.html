Overall Call

Bearish — sell on rise, oversold but broken

Every input except one points the same way this morning, and the one exception is a technical condition rather than a catalyst. GIFT Nifty was quoted at 23,490, down 62.20 points (−0.26%), pointing to a fourth consecutive lower open. The Nifty closed Wednesday around 23,430, down about 0.86%, and the Sensex around 74,760 — below the 75,000 handle, closing near the low of the day’s range. The structure is now a confirmed lower-high, lower-low formation on both daily and intraday charts, with a bearish daily candle.

Crude is the whole story and it has crossed the line that mattered. Brent broke through $100 on Wednesday and was trading near $101.4 in early Thursday dealing, after a roughly 2.9% move; WTI was up around 4.5% near $97. The driver is the widening US–Iran conflict around the Strait of Hormuz — reportedly the largest wave of attacks on shipping yet — compounded by Houthi strikes on Saudi energy infrastructure. Roughly a fifth of world seaborne petroleum trade moves through that strait. For an economy importing the overwhelming majority of its crude, a triple-digit Brent is simultaneously an inflation problem, a current-account problem and a corporate-margin problem. There is no version of this that is good for Indian equities.

Wall Street offered no cover and bonds are making it worse. The Dow fell 0.77% to 52,381.02, the Nasdaq 0.64% to 26,253.34 and the S&P 500 0.48% to 7,636.46. The 10-year Treasury yield held at 4.84% — elevated energy prices and firm yields together is the specific combination that drains risk appetite from emerging markets. The dollar index at 98.74 is steady, removing even the mild currency tailwind that was in place yesterday.

Asia is selling, not bidding. Hang Seng and Kospi were down as much as 1.5%, with the Nikkei off close to 1%. Yesterday’s AI-semiconductor bid that lifted Korea and Japan has reversed. Unlike yesterday, there is now no regional divergence to explain away — Asia and India are falling on the same thing.

The one constructive input is that the RSI has reached oversold. That matters less than it did yesterday, because the index has now broken its previous swing low rather than held it. An oversold reading inside a breakdown is a condition for a bounce, not a reason to expect one. Note also that India VIX rose 6.81% to 11.92 — still a low absolute level, but the direction has turned and volatility is now expanding alongside the breakdown rather than staying flat. That is a less benign picture than Wednesday’s.

Domestic flows remain the floor, and the floor is still holding. FPIs were net sellers of ₹582.99 crore in cash on Wednesday — heavier than Tuesday’s ₹123 crore, but still modest. DIIs bought ₹1,509.04 crore. Domestic buying continues to absorb foreign selling comfortably. This is a macro-driven grind, not distribution.

Net: the derivatives structure supports a sell-on-rise bias until the index produces a higher high and higher low. 23,550/75,300 is the line — below it, weak sentiment continues and the path opens to 23,300–23,200 / 74,300–74,000. The pivot to watch intraday is 23,500: holding it allows a meaningful recovery attempt; losing 23,400 triggers the next leg of correction. Bias down; conviction moderate-to-high, higher than yesterday because support has now actually failed.

What flips the call. To bullish: a credible Hormuz ceasefire or safe-passage arrangement actually being signed, Brent falling back below $95, or the Nifty reclaiming and closing above 23,550/75,300 — which would open a pullback to 23,650–23,700 / 75,500–75,800. A soft US CPI on Friday would do the same job. To more decisively bearish: a sustained break of 23,400 (Bank Nifty losing 55,800 confirms it, opening 55,400), Brent pushing toward $110 on a genuine supply interruption rather than a risk premium, or a hot CPI print that hardens Fed hike expectations. Confirming the base case: a gap-down that fails to reclaim 23,550, crude holding $100–102 without fresh escalation, and a close inside 23,400–23,550 as desks refuse to add risk before Friday.

Global Markets

The US session was an oil session, again. Stocks closed lower across the board on Wednesday as crude soared past $100 and investors de-risked ahead of Friday’s inflation print. Unlike Tuesday — when the Dow’s heavy loss against a resilient Nasdaq marked this out as a cyclical and rate-sensitivity problem — Wednesday’s declines were far more evenly spread (−0.77% / −0.64% / −0.48%). That broadening is the notable change: the market has stopped discriminating and started simply reducing exposure.

Asia has turned over. Asian stocks slid on Thursday on the widening shipping war, with Hong Kong and Korea down as much as 1.5% and Japan off nearly 1%. Yesterday’s semiconductor-led strength in Korea has fully unwound. For India this removes the awkward position of falling while the region rose — but it replaces it with a uniformly negative regional tape, which is worse.

Rates and the dollar are both unhelpful. The 10-year Treasury at 4.8406% and the dollar index at 98.74 are both holding steady at levels that tighten conditions for emerging markets. Yesterday’s soft-dollar consolation is gone.

Europe is a look-ahead. The FTSE 100 and DAX open after this briefing, inheriting a falling Asian tape, a firm dollar, crude above $100 and Treasury yields at 4.84%. On those inputs the bias is lower, with energy the likely sole bright spot. European futures could not be confirmed at the time of writing.

The support broke. The Nifty slipped below its previous swing low on the daily chart and the Sensex closed below 75,000 near the bottom of the day’s range — a short-term structural breakdown rather than a drift. The lower-high, lower-low sequence is intact on both daily and intraday timeframes, and Wednesday produced a bearish daily candle that argues for continuation.

The levels that matter today. 23,550 / 75,300 is immediate resistance; while the index trades below it, weak sentiment is expected to persist, with downside to 23,300–23,200 / 74,300–74,000. Above 23,550/75,300, a bounce can extend to 23,650–23,700 / 75,500–75,800. On the index itself, holding 23,500 keeps a recovery alive; below 23,400 the correction extends. For the Sensex, support sits at 74,500–74,600 with the broader range seen as 74,500–75,200.

Bank Nifty is the weaker half of the market. Four consecutive negative closes, a bearish candle with an upper wick indicating no buying strength, daily RSI below 40, and trading below both its 20-day and 50-day EMAs. It closed below 56,500 and is now at the lower band of a ten-week 56,000–58,700 range. 55,900–55,800 is the crucial support; a sustained break opens 55,400, with a weekly breach of 56,000 pointing to 55,300 and 54,800. Resistance is 56,800–56,900, above which a pullback to 57,300 becomes possible.

Flows: foreign selling up, domestic buying up more. Provisional NSE data shows FPIs net sellers of ₹582.99 crore on Wednesday against DII net purchases of ₹1,509.04 crore. Foreign selling has roughly quintupled from Tuesday’s ₹123 crore but remains small in absolute terms, and domestic institutions are more than absorbing it.

Sectors. IT led Wednesday’s decline, with Infosys, HCLTech and Tech Mahindra among the major losers — a rate-sensitive, US-demand-exposed group reacting to 4.84% Treasury yields. The $100 level is gone. Brent broke through the psychologically critical handle on Wednesday and edged higher to around $101.4 in early Thursday trade. WTI’s 4.5% single-day move is the more violent of the two and points to a supply-disruption premium being repriced rather than a demand story.

The driver is the Strait of Hormuz, and the escalation is in shipping. The latest leg is reported as the biggest wave of attacks on commercial shipping in the widening US–Iran conflict, alongside Houthi strikes on Saudi energy infrastructure. Roughly a fifth of global seaborne petroleum trade transits the strait; analysts and federal agencies expect the bottleneck to keep straining distribution. A secondary support is Chinese buying of African, Canadian and Latin American grades as Beijing restocks depleted crude and fuel inventories.

Why this dominates the Indian call. India imports the large majority of its crude. Triple-digit Brent feeds straight into CPI, widens the current-account deficit, pressures the rupee, squeezes margins for oil-marketing companies, paints, tyres, aviation and chemicals, and constrains the RBI’s room to ease. Every other input in this briefing is secondary to where Brent settles.

Gold is doing its job, quietly. Spot edged higher to above $4,414 on one feed, with others showing roughly $4,400 after a gain of about 1% on Wednesday. Call the level approximate. The move is modest relative to the scale of the geopolitical escalation, which suggests the safe-haven bid is steady accumulation rather than panic.

The drivers are layered. Near-term: Hormuz risk and equity weakness. Offsetting: a steady dollar at 98.74 and 10-year Treasury yields at 4.84% both cap the upside, since a firm dollar and high real yields raise the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset. Structurally, the 2026 bull case rests on record central-bank buying, persistent global inflation and flat mine supply growth of 1–2%.

Context worth keeping. Gold’s all-time high was $5,597 on 29 January 2026 — today’s level is well below that peak despite the current turmoil, which is a useful caution against reading the present move as a blow-off. On the flow side, global gold ETF assets rose 16% month-on-month to about $615 billion with roughly $18 billion of inflows in August, with China, India and Japan driving renewed Asian appetite.

Major Events to Watch (India & USA)

United States

  • August CPI — Friday 11 September, 8:30 a.m. ET. This is the week’s hinge. Consensus is around 3.4% headline year-on-year, unchanged from July and well above the Fed’s 2% target, with core at about 2.5%. The components in focus are shelter, energy, core goods and services, and residual tariff pass-through. With Brent above $100, the energy contribution is the line item that could surprise hawkishly — and a hot print would compound the pressure on emerging-market equities rather than offset it.
  • US–Iran / Strait of Hormuz. The ceasefire has been abandoned and military exchanges are ongoing. This is a live, headline-driven variable that can reprice crude, yields and equities within minutes, in either direction. Any credible move toward a safe-passage arrangement is the single largest upside risk to the bearish call.
  • Treasury yields at 4.84%. Not an event, but the level to watch. A further rise tightens global conditions; a retreat would relieve pressure on Indian IT and rate-sensitives.

India

  • Defence procurement. Preliminary government approval for military purchases of roughly $11 billion is the one clearly positive domestic catalyst in the market, and it lifted BEL and peers on Wednesday. Order-flow follow-through is the thing to track.
  • Crude pass-through to policy. With Brent above $100, the inflation and current-account arithmetic deteriorates and the RBI’s room to cut narrows. Commentary from the central bank on imported inflation is worth watching closely. Note: no MPC decision was confirmed as scheduled for today — do not read this as a meeting-day flag.
  • SEBI enforcement. The regulator lifted its trading ban on a JPMorgan unit in the index-manipulation matter following a ₹2.96 crore deposit — relevant to derivatives-market sentiment and liquidity.
  • Primary market. The Veegaland Developers IPO opened today. Several names including ZEE, Apex Frozen, Power Mech and TVS Srichakra turn ex-dividend today.
