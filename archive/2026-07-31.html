Overall Call

Bullish

This is a genuine, broad, earnings-driven risk rally — and for once India walks in with the good news still ahead of it, not behind it. Microsoft and Amazon both delivered stellar quarters. Microsoft surged roughly 15.5%. That carried Wall Street on Thursday: the Nasdaq Composite settled 2.78% higher, the S&P 500 1.66% and the Dow 1.19%, more than erasing Wednesday's hawkish-Fed drop. Asia then took the baton and ran much further than the US did.

The Asian move is the headline, and it is extraordinary. South Korea's Kospi jumped about 15% — a record single-day gain — with SK Hynix up roughly 25% and Samsung Electronics up about 20%. Japan's Nikkei 225 was up 5.35% and China's CSI 100 up 1.83% in early deals. A 15% move in a major national index is not a normal risk-on session; it is a re-rating of the entire memory and AI-hardware complex on the read-through from US hyperscaler capex.

Crude and gold are both cooperating, which is the part that matters most for India. Oil extended its decline — Brent quoted around $88.18, down about 0.95% — after a Saudi-led coalition proposal to strengthen Red Sea defence cooperation eased the supply-disruption fear that spiked crude nearly 7% two days ago. Gold slipped to roughly $4,086/oz, down about 0.4%, as safe-haven demand unwound. Falling crude plus falling gold plus rising equities is the cleanest risk-on signature there is, and a net energy importer is on the right side of it.

Why the call is bullish but not euphoric. The GIFT Nifty read is conspicuously modest relative to the cues: futures quoted at 24,447, up 89 points, roughly +0.26%. That is a mildly positive open, not a gap-up chasing a 15% Kospi. The reason is structural — the Nifty 50 has almost no semiconductor-manufacturing weight. India participates in this rally through IT services sentiment and general risk appetite, not through the chips actually being re-rated. Expect the Nifty IT pack and large-cap financials to lead, and expect the index to lag Asia badly on a percentage basis. Consensus base case is a flat-to-positive start around 24,340–24,360.

Net: a firm open, IT-led, with cheaper crude quietly supporting OMCs, paints, tyres, aviation and logistics after two days of the opposite. The heavy domestic earnings slate — ITC, Maruti Suzuki, Sun Pharma, Bajaj Finserv, Indian Oil — is more likely to set the afternoon than the global tape is.

What flips the call: turns sideways or negative if the Asian melt-up reverses intraday (a 15% single-day move invites profit-taking and Korea specifically could give a large chunk back), if crude turns back above $92 on any Red Sea headline reversal, if the Nifty fails to hold 24,300 through the first hour, or if ITC and Maruti miss badly enough to sour the domestic tone. Turns more strongly bullish if the Nifty clears 24,450–24,500 with volume, if FIIs extend the buying seen Wednesday, or if crude breaks under $85 and the rupee firms from ~95.6.

Safe-haven demand is unwinding, as it should be on a day like this. Spot gold eased to roughly $4,086 per troy ounce, down about 0.42%, after cresting $4,100 earlier in the week when the Fed held rates steady. Gold and silver futures were both marginally lower in Asian hours.

The drivers are pulling in opposite directions and roughly cancelling. Supporting gold: the Fed's hold, and a soft dollar — the greenback fell sharply earlier in the week on suspicion that Japan intervened again to strengthen the yen. Pressuring gold: three FOMC dissents in favour of a hike, which pushes real-rate expectations the wrong way for a non-yielding asset, plus a broad risk-on tape that gives money better places to be. Net, gold is drifting rather than trending.
