Overall Call

Sideways with a bearish tilt · high volatility

The base case is a choppy, range-bound week that leans lower on any bad headline, not a clean trend in either direction. Three things pull down: Brent sitting near $98 after a ~10% weekly surge on US–Iran escalation, the rupee near record lows at ~96.4/USD, and FIIs having sold ~₹7,182 cr in the cash market last week. Two things cushion: DIIs absorbed all of it (~₹8,638 cr of buying), and Friday's Pakistan/China-brokered peace-talk reports knocked ~4% off crude intraday and gave GIFT Nifty a positive lead.

For India specifically, Nifty enters at 23,767 after five straight losing sessions and a 2.33% weekly drop, sitting below 24,000 and its short-term moving averages. That is a weak structure. Expect a 23,200–24,200 range; sustained trade above 24,000 is the minimum needed to argue the correction is over.

What flips the call: bullish if crude breaks decisively below $90 on genuine de-escalation and the Fed sounds dovish on Wednesday; bearish if Brent reclaims $100, the rupee makes new lows, or Big Tech capex guidance re-triggers the AI selloff that erased ~$800bn of Magnificent Seven value on Thursday.

US. Friday was a stabilisation, not a recovery. The Dow and S&P clawed back while the Nasdaq slipped again as the chip complex stayed under pressure — Intel fell ~6.5% despite an earnings beat and SanDisk dropped ~11% with the memory names. Brent touched $100 intraday during the week — a level not seen since April — before fading nearly 4% on Friday to around $96.8 at the lows on reports that Pakistan, with Chinese backing, is trying to revive US–Iran negotiations. Even after that pullback, it's a third consecutive weekly gain and Brent is up ~30% over the past month.

The supply story is genuinely disrupted, not just a risk premium. US Central Command ran a 13th consecutive night of strikes on Iran. Houthi forces hit two Saudi tankers in the Red Sea — the first spread of ship attacks beyond the Strait of Hormuz vicinity, opening a second front on Saudi Arabia's alternative export route. Asian buyers are discussing rerouting Saudi crude via Suez and around Africa. Separately, the Caspian Pipeline Consortium suspended Black Sea loadings after tanker attacks, choking roughly 80% of Kazakhstan's exports. The US says the Strait remains open with American support; Trump has threatened "major military punishment" and floated a "massive attack" on Iran.

Why this matters most for India: a sustained hold above $90 widens the current account deficit, feeds through to inflation, and effectively closes the RBI's rate-cut window. The White House exempted some energy products from the new tariffs precisely because of this inflation channel.

Gold's week was a recovery, not a breakout. It dipped below $4,000 early (low around $3,982), then climbed back above $4,100 midweek as Treasury yields eased and the dollar lost momentum, before settling near $4,050. Different feeds put Friday's spot between roughly $4,010 and $4,056 depending on the snapshot — treat the level as approximate.

The notable thing is what didn't happen. With US strikes on Iran in their 13th night and Brent touching $100, gold barely moved. Firm Treasury yields and a resilient dollar (DXY ~101.5) capped the safe-haven bid, and traders are pricing the risk that higher energy costs keep the Fed restrictive for longer — which is a headwind for a non-yielding asset. Gold remains well below its January 2026 peak. Wednesday's Fed decision is the near-term swing factor.

Major Events to Watch

USA

  • FOMC, Tue–Wed 28–29 July. Statement 2:00pm ET Wednesday, Chair Kevin Warsh's presser at 2:30pm. The single biggest scheduled risk event of the week — the question is whether the oil-driven inflation impulse shifts the guidance hawkish.
  • New Section 301 tariffs are live. Took effect Friday 24 July, replacing the expired temporary duties after the Supreme Court struck down the emergency-powers version. Rates of 10%–12.5% across ~60 partners covering ~99% of US imports. Some energy products exempted.
  • Data: Durable goods and Dallas Fed Mfg (Mon); JOLTS, Consumer Confidence, advance goods trade balance (Wed); ADP employment, Q2 GDP revision, pending home sales (Thu); PCE inflation, personal income/spending, Chicago PMI, Challenger job cuts (Fri).
  • Middle East. Whether the Pakistan/China-mediated US–Iran channel produces anything is the biggest unscheduled variable for both crude and equities.

India

  • India's tariff rate set at 10% under the new US measures — below the 12.5% floated in June, and generic pharma, smartphones, steel, aluminium and auto parts are excluded. India said Saturday it will keep engaging to conclude the bilateral agreement. This is a relatively benign outcome versus expectations, and it sits alongside the earlier 18% reciprocal-tariff framework that has been supporting auto and electronics exporters.
  • Rupee. Near record lows around 96.4/USD. Any further slide compounds the crude import bill and pressures FII flows.
  • Monthly F&O expiry and rollover activity this week.
  • Month-end data into the following week: fiscal deficit, infrastructure output, then HSBC Manufacturing PMI and GST collections in early August.
