The de-escalation trade is dead — Brent is back at $99.5 and the Nifty is now sitting on the last support that matters

No truce materialised. The US and Iran continued to exchange fire around the Strait through Tuesday, Brent went up roughly 1.5% rather than down, and Indian equities fell for a second straight session. The de-escalation thesis has been withdrawn from this briefing until something is actually signed.

Overall Call

Bearish bias — but oversold at support

Three things line up on the bearish side this morning, and only one on the other. GIFT Nifty is quoted at 23,657, down 97 points (a second feed has 23,660.50, down 89.10), pointing to a third consecutive lower open. The Nifty closed Tuesday at 23,635.10, down 144.05 points (−0.61%) and the Sensex at 75,577.58, down 555.23 (−0.73%). That leaves the index resting directly on 23,600 — the level flagged as crucial support for the last two sessions.

Crude is the problem and it has got worse, not better. Brent jumped 1.5% to about $99.5 on Tuesday as US–Iran hostilities around the Strait of Hormuz intensified, with WTI up around 3% and now in its longest winning streak since March. For an economy that imports the overwhelming majority of its crude, Brent knocking on $100 is simultaneously an inflation problem, a current-account problem and a corporate-margin problem. This is the single dominant variable for Indian equities right now, and it is pointing the wrong way.

Wall Street gave India no cover. The Dow fell 1.18% to 52,786.07 — a 628-point drop, and notably heavier than the S&P 500 at −0.58% (7,673.52) or the Nasdaq at −0.32% (26,421.41). That spread is the signature of an energy-cost and rate-sensitivity problem hitting old-economy names, not a broad tech de-rating. The 10-year Treasury settled at 4.8%. US futures this morning are flat to mixed.

The one genuinely constructive input is that the tape is stretched. The Nifty’s RSI is at 34, sliding toward oversold, and the index is arriving at 23,600 having already given up ground on five of the last several sessions. Technicians are explicitly flagging the possibility of a bounce from 23,600–23,500. Note too that India VIX is only 11.14 (up 4.31%) — this is an orderly grind lower, not a panic. Low VIX into a support test usually means the selling is mechanical rather than forced.

Asia is up but for reasons India cannot borrow. Kospi is up 1.36–1.75% on SK Hynix and Samsung, Nikkei up 0.4%, Hang Seng little changed. That is an AI-semiconductor bid. It lifts Korean and Japanese tech; it does very little for an Indian market whose problem is an oil import bill.

Domestic flows have thinned on both sides. On Tuesday FPIs were net sellers of just ₹123.19 crore in cash while DIIs bought ₹1,349.64 crore. Both numbers are small. The floor is still there, but the selling is not aggressive either — which fits a market drifting down on macro rather than being dumped.

Net: expect an open around 23,540–23,600 into a market that is oversold, sitting on its last defended level, with no positive catalyst before US CPI. The honest read is that 23,600 either holds today or the next leg is 23,450. Bias down; conviction moderate, because the bounce setup is real.

What flips the call. To bullish: a credible Hormuz ceasefire or safe-passage agreement actually being ratified, Brent breaking back under $95, or the Nifty closing above 23,750/75,800 — which would open a pullback to 23,850–23,900. To more decisively bearish: a sustained break of 23,500 (Bank Nifty losing 56,300 would confirm it), Brent through $100, or a hot US CPI print hardening the Fed hike odds beyond the ~60% currently priced. Confirming the base case: a gap-down that finds bids near 23,550, crude holding $97–100 without a fresh headline, and a close inside 23,550–23,750 as desks stay light into CPI.

Global Markets

The US session was an energy-cost session. Stocks fell across the board on Tuesday as oil climbed and investors positioned ahead of this week’s inflation print. The Dow’s 1.18% drop against the Nasdaq’s 0.32% is the number to look at: this was cyclicals and rate-sensitives being marked down, not a technology unwind. WTI rose for a sixth straight day, its longest run since March.

Asia is bid, but narrowly. Most Asia-Pacific markets advanced in early trade on continued enthusiasm for AI-linked names, with Korea leading on SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics. Japan is modestly higher and Hong Kong is roughly flat. This is a sector story, not a regional risk-on, and India has very little exposure to the thing doing the lifting.

The dollar is soft. The dollar index is at 98.15, close to its lowest in almost two weeks. That is a mild positive for emerging-market flows and for the rupee’s crude arithmetic — and it is one of the few things working in India’s favour this morning.

Europe is a look-ahead. The FTSE 100 and DAX open after this briefing, inheriting a firm Asian tape, a soft dollar and crude near $99.5. European futures could not be confirmed at the time of writing.

The technical picture is uniformly negative, and unusually consistent across desks. A long bear candle formed on Tuesday’s daily chart, the index is holding a lower-top formation intraday, and it is trading below its key moving averages. Kotak Securities puts immediate resistance at 23,750/75,800 and sees a retest of 23,500–23,450 / 75,200–75,000 if that caps. HDFC Securities notes the index is placed at crucial support around 23,600 with no sign of upside recovery yet, but flags a possible bounce from 23,600–23,500. Choice Equity Broking has Sensex support at 75,000–75,300 and needs a sustained hold above 76,000 for a meaningful recovery.

Bank Nifty is the weaker half of the market. It has slipped below its 20-, 50- and 100-day EMAs and closed under 57,000 for a second consecutive bearish candle. Support is 56,400–56,300; a sustained move below 56,300 opens 55,800. Resistance is 57,000–57,100, coinciding with the 100-day EMA. The broader consolidation range is 56,500–58,700. Given banks’ index weight, a Bank Nifty breakdown is what would turn today’s soft open into a trend day.

Flows are small on both sides. Tuesday’s provisional NSE data: FPIs net sellers of ₹123.19 crore, DIIs net buyers of ₹1,349.64 crore. Separately, FIIs are carrying a large net short position in index futures — reported at around 2.6 lakh contracts — which cuts both ways: it is bearish positioning, but it is also fuel for a short-covering bounce off 23,600.

Strategy note doing the rounds. Geojit’s Vinod Nair argues the five-to-six-month outperformance of mid- and small-caps will be hard to sustain and favours large-caps, non-equity ETFs, and defensive or deep-value pockets. That is consistent with the tape: this is a market where the marginal buyer is domestic and defensive.

Primary market is heavy again. Opening today: Rentomojo, Asset Reconstruction (ARCIL), Manipal Payment & Identity Solutions, Steamhouse India, LCC Projects and Karamtara Engineering. Kanohar Electricals, Prasol Chemicals and Glass Wall Systems enter day two; Pranav Constructions (₹351.03 crore) and Apana Logistics (₹34.14 crore) enter their final day. Six mainboard openings in one session is a meaningful liquidity draw from a secondary market already short of buyers.

Crude Oil

Brent roughly $99.3, and the direction of risk is up. The September contract is quoted at about $99.26 this morning, essentially flat (−0.05%) after Tuesday’s 1.5% jump to around $99.5. WTI rose about 3% to roughly $94.24 on Tuesday and has now climbed for six consecutive sessions — its longest run since a seven-day streak in March. These are the highest levels since July.

The driver is unresolved military conflict, not fundamentals. The US and Iran continued exchanging fire around the Strait of Hormuz through Tuesday, raising supply concerns for a waterway that carries roughly a fifth of global oil in peacetime. Iran has floated a temporary safe-passage arrangement via Oman while simultaneously warning that ships remain at risk — which is why the market is not pricing relief. Brent is now roughly 40% above pre-war levels.

Why this dominates the Indian call. Every $5 on Brent tightens the current account, feeds imported inflation, and squeezes margins across paints, aviation, tyres, chemicals and downstream OMCs. It also constrains the RBI. Until crude turns, Indian equities are trading with a hand tied behind their back — and yesterday’s lesson is not to price a truce before one exists.

Gold

Around $4,400–4,450 an ounce, and rates are still beating geopolitics. Gold opened Tuesday near $4,451.60, down 0.6% from Friday, and traded down through the session with prints as low as $4,391 quoted intraday. Gold and silver futures are both softer this morning, down about 0.44% and 0.42% respectively. The spread between providers is unusually wide today — treat any single level as approximate.

The signal is in what gold is refusing to do. There is an active military confrontation at the world’s most important oil chokepoint, crude is at a two-month high, and gold is falling. Traders are pricing roughly a 60% chance of a Fed rate hike at the 15–16 September meeting after the strong jobs report. Higher rates raise the opportunity cost of a non-yielding asset, and that is currently overwhelming the safe-haven bid. A soft CPI print this week would likely flip that quickly.

Note the tension: Indian retail demand is reported to be picking up on safe-haven buying even as the dollar price falls — a rupee-versus-dollar-price divergence worth keeping in mind before reading Indian gold headlines as a global signal.

Major Events to Watch (India & USA)

  • US CPI — later this week. The last inflation read before the Fed decides, and the reason positioning stays light. Most reporting points to Friday 11 September; the exact date could not be confirmed before deadline — verify against the BLS calendar. This is the highest-impact scheduled item on the list.
  • FOMC — 15–16 September, with an updated Summary of Economic Projections and press conference. Markets are pricing roughly a 60% probability of a hike. A hawkish outcome is a direct negative for emerging-market flows.
  • Strait of Hormuz — ongoing US–Iran exchanges. Unscheduled, live, and capable of landing during Indian hours. Both the largest downside risk and, if a safe-passage deal is actually signed, the largest upside catalyst.
  • BRICS Summit, New Delhi. Motilal Oswal flags it as a domestic focus item alongside crude. Watch for energy, trade and payments announcements with market relevance rather than the communiqué itself.
  • Rupee and the oil import bill. With Brent near $100 and the dollar index soft, the rupee’s behaviour is the cleanest single read on how much of the crude shock is actually landing on the Indian economy.
  • Domestic supply. Six mainboard IPOs open today and the NSE’s own listing process is live post-SEBI approval. Primary-market absorption is a real drag on secondary-market breadth this week.

NSE Earnings Highlights

There is no Nifty 50 earnings event today — the market is between reporting cycles. Q1 FY27 results closed in late July and August; Q2 FY27 numbers are not due until October. That vacuum is exactly why crude, the Fed and flows are setting direction almost unopposed this week. What follows is corporate news flow, not results.

  • Biocon — True North-owned Active Pine is reported to be selling up to 1.66 crore shares (about 1% of paid-up equity) via block deal, offer size around ₹638.4 crore at a floor price of ₹385. Expect supply pressure on the stock.
  • TCS — won a ₹122.6 crore contract from the Odisha government to implement OSWAS 3.0, extending the partnership by roughly six years, covering workflow automation, analytics, security and AI on a common platform. Small in revenue terms; useful as a signal on domestic government IT spend.
  • Bank of Baroda — plans to divest up to 76,90,375 NSE shares, about 35% of its holding, through an offer-for-sale as part of the NSE IPO, subject to approvals. A visible monetisation event tied to the exchange’s listing.
  • Coforge — Chairman OP Bhatt has resigned following an internal audit review. Governance headlines of this kind usually get punished first and analysed later.
  • Also in focus: NLC India, Innovision, GE Shipping, Moschip and Raymond. A batch of names including Century Plyboards, Force Motors, GNFC, JBM Auto and Shree Digvijay Cement trade ex-dividend today — expect mechanical price adjustments that are not real moves.
