Overall Call

Sideways — with a relief-bounce bias

The single most important thing that happened overnight was not equity news. The US Treasury said on Wednesday it will more than double the size of its buybacks of long-dated government debt. Long yields fell sharply on the announcement, and that one move reversed the exact pressure that had been grinding global equities lower all week — the 30-year yield had touched a 19-year high only a session earlier. Wall Street snapped a three-day losing streak on the back of it.

That gives the Indian market its first genuinely supportive overnight setup in more than a week. The Nifty has fallen for seven consecutive sessions, closing Wednesday at 24,078.30 (−0.32%), its longest losing run in roughly a year and down about 2.1% over the stretch. GIFT Nifty is pointing modestly higher this morning — around 24,219, up roughly 30 points — which reads as bargain-hunting rather than conviction.

Why the call stops short of bullish. Two things cap it. Brent is still parked near $91 with the lapsed US–Iran memorandum unreplaced, which keeps the import bill and the inflation path uncomfortable. And the Nifty has slipped below its 50-day moving average, with 24,000 now the level everyone is watching rather than a level far below the tape. A yield-driven relief bounce that leaves crude and the technical damage untouched is a bounce, not a turn. Domestic institutions did the heavy lifting again on Wednesday — DIIs bought roughly ₹3,974 crore against FII net buying of only about ₹408 crore — which is support, but it is one-legged support.

What flips the call. To bullish: a decisive close above the 24,230–24,250 resistance zone with positive breadth and the Nifty reclaiming its 50-DMA, ideally alongside Brent slipping back under $88 — that combination would confirm the yield relief is being treated as durable rather than technical. To bearish: a break and close below 24,000, which opens 23,950–23,900 and then 23,750; or Brent pushing back above $95; or long US yields retracing the buyback rally, which would tell you the bond market read the Treasury’s move as a supply problem rather than a solution.

Global Markets

Wall Street is the settled part of the picture and it turned. All three major indices closed higher on Wednesday, ending a three-session slide, after the Treasury’s buyback announcement pulled long yields down sharply. The gains were narrow rather than broad — health care and cyclicals did the work while tech lagged — and the standout single stock was Moderna, up around 176% on positive late-stage melanoma vaccine results from its programme with Merck.

Indian Markets

Wednesday was a seventh straight down session, but a mild one. The Nifty 50 closed at 24,078.30, down 76.60 points or 0.32%. The Sensex closed at 76,909.68, down 325.78 points or 0.42%. The cumulative damage over the seven sessions is about 2.1% — a grind rather than a break, driven by elevated crude, rising global bond yields and broad-based caution rather than any domestic shock.

Flows. FIIs were net buyers of roughly ₹408 crore in the cash segment on Wednesday — a small figure, but a positive one after a stretch of selling. DIIs were net buyers of roughly ₹3,974 crore, continuing to absorb supply. The asymmetry between the two is the thing to watch: domestic money is holding the market up, and foreign money has not yet committed.

Levels. The index has slipped below its 50-day moving average. Immediate resistance sits at 24,230–24,250. Support is 24,000, with the 23,950–23,900 zone below it; a sustained break of that band opens 23,750 and then 23,600.

Crude Oil

Crude remains the main domestic headwind. Brent is trading around $91–$92 — sources this morning span roughly $91.2 to $91.9, so treat the level as approximate rather than precise. WTI was last around $85.3.

The driver is unchanged and unresolved: the 60-day US–Iran memorandum lapsed earlier this week with no replacement, leaving a geopolitical risk premium embedded in the price. For an import-dependent economy this is the input that connects most directly to the rupee, the current account and the inflation path — which is why an equity relief rally driven purely by US yields only goes so far. A move back under $88 would materially change the Indian setup; a move above $95 would do the opposite.

Gold

Gold had a strong session, jumping more than $130 as Treasury yields retreated on the buyback news and the dollar softened. Spot is being quoted in the $4,475–$4,518 range depending on the feed — the levels disagree across providers, so call it approximately $4,500 rather than quoting a single figure as precise.

The move is a clean expression of the same overnight story that lifted equities: lower real yields reduce the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset. Persistent geopolitical tension around Iran is providing a second, independent bid. Note that gold and equities rallying together on a yield decline is coherent, not contradictory — but it also means both are leaning on the same single catalyst.

Major Events to Watch — India & USA

  • July FOMC minutes (released Wednesday, now being digested). The Committee left rates unchanged at 3.50–3.75%, but the decision drew three dissents — Logan, Hammack and Kashkari all voted for a 25bp hike. A hawkish dissent bloc of that size is unusual and cuts against the market’s easing assumptions.
  • Treasury long-bond buyback programme. The follow-through matters more than the announcement. Whether long yields hold their decline through today’s session is the single cleanest read on whether this week’s relief is durable.
  • Jackson Hole, 27–29 August. Theme: “Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy.” Kevin Warsh delivers his first keynote as Fed Chair on Friday 28 August. That is next week, but positioning ahead of it starts now.
  • US–Iran. No replacement framework after the memorandum lapsed. Any credible restart of talks is the fastest route to lower crude; any escalation is the fastest route to $95+.
  • India: no scheduled top-tier domestic macro release was identified for today inside the research window. The domestic tape is being driven by global inputs — crude, yields and flows — rather than by local data.

NSE Earnings Highlights

National Stock Exchange (NSE) — Q1 FY27. The exchange reported revenue from operations of ₹4,560.41 crore, up 13.09% year on year, and PAT of ₹3,120.08 crore, up 6.71%. EBITDA came in at ₹4,332.03 crore (+10.33%), but the EBITDA margin slipped to 94.99% from 97.36% — a 237 basis point decline, reflecting higher operating expenses and strategic investment.
