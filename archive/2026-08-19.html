Overall Call

Bearish tilt — range still intact

Three things are pushing the same direction at once, and that is unusual enough to respect. The 60-day US–Iran memorandum expired on Monday with no replacement and no appetite from Washington to extend it. Brent has climbed for four straight sessions toward $92. And the US 30-year Treasury yield touched 5.327% on Tuesday — a 19-year high, the highest since April 2007. Higher oil and higher long-end yields are the two inputs an import-dependent, foreign-flow-sensitive market least wants to see move together.

The Nifty has now fallen for six consecutive sessions, closing Tuesday at 24,154.90 after a 0.55% drop. It opened essentially unchanged this morning at 24,152, exactly as GIFT Nifty had signalled — a 20-point discount pointing to a flat start. A flat open after six down days is not a bottom; it is an absence of new sellers. Breadth at the open was still negative, 1,406 declining against 1,244 advancing.

There is one genuine bright spot worth naming. IT led the tape at the open — HCL Tech up over 1.2%, with Infosys, Tech Mahindra, Eternal and Sun Pharma also higher. A weaker rupee and a hunt for defensives explain most of that. Financials went the other way, with Bajaj Finserv and Bajaj Finance the notable drags, which is what a 19-year-high long bond yield does to rate-sensitive lenders.

What flips the call. To bullish: any credible restart of US–Iran talks or a Hormuz reopening framework that pulls Brent back under $85 — that one change repairs the import bill, the rupee and the inflation path simultaneously. Failing that, a close above 24,400 with visible institutional buying and positive breadth would end the six-session sequence convincingly. To bearish outright: Brent breaking and holding above $95, a decisive close below 24,000, or the 30-year yield pushing through 5.50% — any of which would turn an orderly drift into a repricing. Thursday’s FOMC minutes and the start of Jackson Hole are the scheduled catalysts.

Global Markets

Wall Street is the settled part of the picture and it was not a good session. Tuesday closed lower for a third straight day: the Nasdaq Composite fell about 1.3% as AI and chip names sold off again, the S&P 500 lost roughly 0.7%, and the Dow held up best at around −0.2%. The proximate cause was the bond market rather than earnings — the 30-year yield hitting 5.327% forces a discount-rate rethink on exactly the long-duration growth names that led this year’s rally.

This is the day’s driving variable. The memorandum of understanding signed in June — the one that gave Washington and Tehran 60 days to convert a ceasefire into a durable settlement — expired on Monday, 18 August with nothing to replace it. President Trump has said he is not interested in extending the interim deal. Brent climbed toward $92 on Tuesday, a fourth consecutive advance, on precisely that.

The mechanism for India is direct and unhelpful: crude is the largest single import line, so a sustained $92 Brent widens the trade deficit, pressures the rupee, feeds into the inflation path, and squeezes margins across manufacturing, autos and chemicals. It also removes the RBI’s room for manoeuvre at exactly the moment global yields are rising.

The $92 figure is approximate and reflects Tuesday’s move rather than a verified live print. Gold

Gold is trading in the $4,340–4,350 region, with sources clustering around $4,339 and $4,352 an ounce — approximate, as always with spot. That marks a step down from last week’s $4,400-plus levels: bullion eased below $4,400 on Tuesday in a broad metals pullback as global bond yields surged to multi-year highs.

That reaction is the instructive part. Gold usually rallies on the kind of geopolitical escalation currently supporting oil, and it did not. When real yields jump hard enough, the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset overwhelms the safe-haven bid, at least in the short run. Offsetting that, expectations of a Federal Reserve rate hike have faded after a run of soft US data — markets now expect no change in September and no longer fully price an increase by year-end, which puts a floor under the metal.

The read-across: gold declining while oil rises and equities fall is a market pricing inflation-plus-tighter-financial-conditions, not a classic risk-off panic. That is the more uncomfortable of the two regimes for Indian equities, because it offers no monetary relief valve.

NSE Earnings Highlights

The Q1 FY27 season is closed. The SEBI filing deadline was Friday 14 August, so there is no meaningful large-cap results calendar today or for the rest of this week. No Nifty 50 constituent was confirmed as reporting on 19 August within the research window. That absence is itself relevant: with no company-specific flow to trade, the index is left to react purely to macro, which is exactly what it is doing.

One notable late-season number, for the exchange itself rather than a listed constituent: NSE reported Q1 FY27 revenue from operations of ₹4,560.41 crore, up 13.09% year on year, with profit after tax of ₹3,120.08 crore against ₹2,923.85 crore — a 6.71% increase. The long-running ₹1,491 crore SEBI colocation matter was settled, with the board approving payment on 30 July 2026. Revenue growing at roughly double the pace of profit is the same pattern the broader Nifty 50 showed this season: healthy top lines, margin compression underneath.

That aggregate picture — revenue growth intact, net profit growth softer on rising costs — is the reason the index has struggled to justify multiple expansion. With Brent near $92 feeding straight into input costs, the squeeze has no obvious self-correction this quarter.
