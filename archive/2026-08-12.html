Overall Call

Sideways

This is a session the market has chosen to sit out, and the reason is calendar rather than conviction. Two consumer inflation prints land after the equity action — India's July CPI at 4:00 pm IST and the US July CPI at 8:30 am ET, roughly 6:00 pm IST. Neither can be traded during the Indian day, and both are large enough to reset the next few sessions. When the two biggest events of the day arrive after the close, the rational thing is a narrow range, and that is exactly what has happened: GIFT Nifty pointed to a tepid open at 24,547, up all of 6 points, the Nifty opened effectively unchanged at 24,472.45, and the Sensex has spent the morning oscillating inside a couple of hundred points either side of flat.

The global tape is a mild drag, not a threat. Wall Street slipped again on Tuesday — the Dow off 0.34%, the S&P 500 off 0.32% and the Nasdaq Composite off 0.60% — a third consecutive session of drift lower from the record close set on 6 August, with Alphabet a notable weight. Asia this morning is genuinely split rather than weak: Japan and South Korea firmly higher, Hong Kong down over 1%, mainland China marginally lower. A split Asia with soft US futures gives the Nifty no directional lead at all.

Crude near $90 is the constraint that caps the upside. Brent's front month is quoted around $89.55, up about 0.7%, extending gains as the prospect of an imminent US–Iran deal on the Strait of Hormuz fades. Tehran now says the US naval blockade must lift before it will reopen the strait; Washington has ruled out anything cementing Iranian control. For a country importing the overwhelming majority of its crude, $90 Brent is a live tax on the current account, the rupee and headline inflation — and it arrives on the same day the RBI's inflation input is published. That combination is why the call is sideways rather than mildly bullish despite an otherwise unremarkable global backdrop.

Under the flat index, stock-level dispersion is unusually high. Godrej Consumer Products is down 10% at the lower circuit after the resignation of MD and CEO Sudhir Sitapati. HDFC Bank has hit a two-year low, down roughly 12% in a month, which matters disproportionately given its index weight. Against that, PSU banks and metals are leading, Adani group names rose up to 3% on US court relief for Gautam Adani, and Gland Pharma has run to a four-year high. Add a 546-company earnings day headlined by Tata Motors, HAL, Grasim and Apollo Hospitals, and the honest description is a quiet index concealing a busy market.

What flips the call: turns bullish if the US core CPI prints soft (0.2% m/m or below), which would take the September Fed hike off the table; if a Hormuz breakthrough headline drops and Brent breaks under $85; if India's July CPI comes in at or below the 4.50% poll, easing RBI pressure; or if the Nifty clears 24,600 with volume. Turns bearish if US core CPI runs 0.4% m/m or hotter and September hike odds firm up; if Brent breaks $92 on a further hardening of Iranian or US positions; if India's CPI overshoots toward 4.7%+; if the HDFC Bank derating drags the wider private-bank complex; or if the Nifty loses 24,350 and the PSU-bank leadership fades.

The Hormuz premium is going back on, not coming off. Brent's August futures were quoted around $89.55, up 0.72%, pressing the $90 handle as hope of an imminent US–Iran agreement drains away. The sequence matters: the two sides signed a memorandum of understanding on 17 June to open the strait to commercial shipping; it collapsed within weeks in a dispute over which routes vessels could use, with Iran insisting on transit through its territorial waters. The US responded with airstrikes and reimposed its naval blockade. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on 4 August that a deal could land "today or tomorrow"; by 10 August there was none, and oil had jumped about 5% as doubt set in. Iran's foreign ministry has since stated that as long as the blockade continues, the conditions to reopen Hormuz do not exist. Both positions have hardened.

Levels are approximate and the spread between feeds is wide. Brent was quoted opening at $87.80 on 11 August and near $89.55 this morning; WTI opened 11 August at $82.34. The Brent–WTI gap implied by those quotes is unusually large, so treat $88–$90 Brent and $82–$84 WTI as working ranges rather than marks. Direction — higher, on deal failure — is the reliable part.

For India: straightforwardly negative, and doubly awkward today. Every dollar on Brent widens the import bill, pressures the rupee and feeds through to headline inflation with a lag — on the same afternoon the July CPI print is published. Oil marketing companies, paints, tyres, aviation and logistics face margin compression; upstream producers such as ONGC and Oil India are the offset.

Gold is firm but drifting, and the level is genuinely contested. One feed puts spot at about $4,368 on 12 August; another had it at $4,397.59 on the afternoon of 11 August US time. That is a roughly $30 spread on the same metal within a day, so the honest statement is that gold is somewhere in the $4,350–$4,400 band. Futures were up 0.34% in Asian hours with silver up 0.49%, and the metal has continued to grind higher through the past week even as the July jobs report missed.

The drivers are unusually two-sided. Supporting gold: an unresolved Middle East supply crisis with a naval blockade in force, which is about as clean a safe-haven case as exists. Pressuring it: a September FOMC where a 25 basis point hike is priced as roughly a coin toss against a hold. A rising-rate path is the standard enemy of a non-yielding asset, and it is the reason gold is edging rather than surging on the geopolitics. Today's US CPI is the swing factor for both legs simultaneously, and expectations are for high volatility through the week's data run — ADP employment, CPI, PPI, jobless claims and preliminary University of Michigan inflation expectations.

  • India July CPI — 4:00 pm IST. A Reuters poll looks for 4.50% year-on-year, up from 4.38%. The level matters less than the surprise: this is a direct input into RBI rate expectations, and it lands on a day Brent is testing $90.
  • US July CPI — 8:30 am ET / ~6:00 pm IST. Headline expected to ease to 3.4% y/y from 3.5%, and about +0.1% m/m. Core forecasts cluster at 2.4–2.5% y/y and roughly +0.32% m/m. The single most consequential number of the day for global risk.
  • September FOMC is a coin toss — toward a hike. This bears repeating because it inverts the usual assumption: after the July jobs report, futures were pricing roughly 50-50 odds that the Fed under chair Kevin Warsh raises by 25bp in September versus holding. An upside CPI surprise strengthens the hike case directly.
  • US–Iran / Strait of Hormuz. The dominant macro variable for Indian equities right now, transmitted through crude. Positions on both sides have hardened; a genuine breakthrough would be a large positive shock for India, and further escalation the reverse.
  • Godrej Consumer leadership. Sudhir Sitapati's resignation as MD and CEO is today's biggest single-stock event and worth following for succession news and any read-across to FMCG governance sentiment.
  • Adani litigation. US court relief for Gautam Adani lifted group stocks up to 3%. Further procedural news moves a large block of index and infrastructure weight.
